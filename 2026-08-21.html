1770 · Australia

James Cook Claims New South Wales

James Cook claimed the unmapped eastern coastline of New Holland for Great Britain, initially calling the territory New Wales.

What happened

In 1770, James Cook charted the unmapped eastern coastline of the continent of New Holland, present-day Australia. Contrary to his official instructions, Cook did not obtain the consent of the Aboriginal inhabitants, who had populated the region for 40,000 to 60,000 years with an estimated population of 250,000 before European arrival. Cook claimed the entire explored coastline as British territory, initially naming the region New Wales before changing it to New South Wales during his return voyage to Britain.

British settlement formally began in January 1788 when Arthur Phillip arrived in Botany Bay commanding the First Fleet of 11 vessels, carrying over 1,000 settlers, including 736 convicts. Finding Botany Bay unsuitable, Phillip relocated the fleet to Port Jackson and established a settlement at Sydney Cove on 26 January 1788, formally proclaiming the colony on 7 February 1788.

What changed after

Governor Arthur Phillip exercised nominal authority over eastern Australia between the 10°37'S and 43°39'S latitudes, encompassing modern New South Wales, Queensland, Victoria, and Tasmania. The initial penal colony was planned as a self-sufficient agricultural settlement, and by 1803, farms surrounding Parramatta, Windsor, and Camden made the colony self-sufficient in grain.

During the 19th century, large sections of New South Wales were detached to create independent British colonies across Australia. In 1825, the colony's western boundary was set at the 129th meridian east, incorporating Van Diemen's Land, Lord Howe Island, and Norfolk Island under its jurisdiction.

Assassination of Benigno Aquino Jr.

Former Philippine senator Benigno Aquino Jr. was shot dead upon returning from exile at Manila International Airport in 1983.

What happened

On August 21, 1983, former Philippine senator Benigno "Ninoy" Aquino Jr. landed at Manila International Airport after three years of self-imposed exile near Boston, Massachusetts. As he was being escorted from his aircraft to a vehicle intended to return him to prison, Aquino was killed by a gunshot to the head on the tarmac apron. Rolando Galman, accused of committing the murder, was also shot dead at the scene.

Aquino had been a prominent political opponent of President Ferdinand Marcos Sr. since his election to the Senate in 1967. Imprisoned at Fort Bonifacio under false charges of subversion following the declaration of martial law in September 1972, Aquino went on a 40-day hunger strike before receiving a death sentence from a military tribunal in November 1977. Marcos permitted him to travel to the United States for heart surgery in May 1980, where Aquino remained until choosing to return in 1983 to seek democratic reforms.

What changed after

Aquino's assassination unified Philippine opposition movements, propelling his widow, Corazon Aquino, into political leadership. In 1986, Marcos called a snap presidential election in which Corazon Aquino ran as the opposition candidate. Massive public protests during the resulting People Power Revolution forced Marcos to resign and flee the country, allowing Corazon Aquino to assume the presidency.

Legal proceedings following the assassination initially resulted in the acquittal of 25 military personnel and one civilian by the Sandiganbayan court under the Marcos administration. A second investigation ordered during Corazon Aquino's presidency led to a retrial in which 16 military personnel were convicted and sentenced to life imprisonment, with the final convicts released from prison in 2009.
