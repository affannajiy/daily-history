U.S. Invasion of Afghanistan

United States and coalition forces launched an invasion of Afghanistan following the September 11 attacks to dismantle al-Qaeda and remove the Taliban.

What happened

Following the September 11 attacks masterminded by al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden, American President George W. Bush demanded that the Taliban immediately extradite bin Laden to the United States and shut down al-Qaeda training camps in Afghanistan. The Taliban refused the demand and requested evidence of bin Laden's guilt before offering to hand him over to a neutral country. The United States rejected these offers and initiated military action.

On October 7, 2001, the United States invasion of Afghanistan began under Operation Enduring Freedom, utilizing air assaults and covert ground operations. Partnering with the anti-Taliban Northern Alliance, American-led coalition forces drove Taliban forces from major population centers across the country and ousted the Taliban-ruled Islamic Emirate.

What changed after

The initial invasion expelled the Taliban from power and enabled the creation of the Afghan Interim Administration, followed by the establishment of the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan in 2004. To maintain security and prevent a Taliban return, the United Nations sanctioned the International Security Assistance Force, which operated until NATO transferred primary security duties to Afghan armed forces in 2014.

The conflict became the longest war in United States military history, surpassing the Vietnam War by six months. The war resulted in an estimated 176,000 to over 212,000 deaths, while millions of Afghans remained displaced internally or as refugees by the time American forces fully withdrew in August 2021.
