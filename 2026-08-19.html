1991 · Foros, Ukraine

August Coup (1991)

Hard‑line Soviet officials seized Gorbachev at his Foros dacha, igniting a three‑day power struggle that hastened the USSR’s collapse.

What happened

In August 1991 hard‑liners of the Communist Party formed the State Committee on the State of Emergency (GKChP) and dispatched KGB agents to detain Soviet President Mikhail Gorbachev at his dacha in the Black Sea resort town of Foros, Ukraine. The coup leaders, including Vice President Gennady Yanayev, announced an emergency state while Gorbachev was placed under house arrest.

Boris Yeltsin, recently elected president of the Russian SFSR, rallied civilian protesters in Moscow and refused to recognise the GKChP. Massive anti‑authoritarian demonstrations, combined with Yeltsin’s defiance, crippled the plotters. Within two days the GKChP collapsed, Gorbachev returned to office, and the coup leaders lost their posts. The failure precipitated the immediate collapse of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union and set the stage for the USSR’s dissolution four months later.

What changed after

The coup’s failure triggered the rapid disintegration of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union, which was formally banned within weeks. Four months later, on 25 December 1991, the Soviet Union formally dissolved, creating fifteen independent republics. Boris Yeltsin emerged as the dominant political figure in the new Russian Federation, while Mikhail Gorbachev’s influence waned dramatically.

New constitutional structures were adopted in the successor states, and the former Soviet military and security apparatus were divided among the independent nations, reshaping the geopolitical landscape of Eurasia.

August Revolution (1945)

The Viet Minh seized vacant administrative buildings in Hanoi, proclaiming the Democratic Republic of Vietnam and ending Japanese occupation.

Viet Minh

What happened

When Japan surrendered in August 1945, the occupying forces handed over several public buildings in Hanoi to the Viet Minh under Ho Chi Minh. The Viet Minh quickly filled the power vacuum, assuming control of the city’s administration and police.

Within weeks Ho Chi Minh proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam on 2 September 1945, establishing a new government that claimed sovereignty over the whole country and set the stage for the ensuing struggle against French re‑occupation.
