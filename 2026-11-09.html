Fall of the Berlin Wall

A premature announcement at a press conference prompted crowds to assemble at Berlin checkpoints until border guards stepped aside.

Who
East German citizens and border guards
What
Checkpoints opened along the Berlin Wall
Where
Berlin, East Germany
When
9 November 1989, late evening hours
What happened

In 1989, East German citizens sought paths to the West after Hungary removed its border fence in May and opened its border at the Pan-European Picnic in August. Thousands of refugees crammed into the West German embassy in Prague, where West German Foreign Minister Hans-Dietrich Genscher announced on 30 September that they were cleared to depart. As defection continued despite the end of the holiday season, long-term East German leader Erich Honecker was ousted in October while successor Egon Krenz attempted to secure loans from West Germany to avoid financial default.

On 9 November 1989, a Politburo press conference prematurely declared that travel restrictions for East Germans were lifted immediately. Hearkening to live news reports across East and West Berlin, crowds gathered at border crossings along the 155-kilometer, four-meter-tall concrete barrier. By late evening, overwhelmed border guards chose not to enforce shooting orders across the mined death strip, opening the checkpoints and allowing citizens to cross freely into West Berlin.

What changed after

After the opening of the checkpoints, East Germans dismantled L-shaped concrete segments of the 155-kilometer barrier, scattering pieces across the world. Following free elections held in the spring of 1990, the Deutsche Mark was introduced to the region in July.

Full political unification occurred on 3 October 1990 when five newly formed states joined the ten existing states of the Federal Republic of Germany, establishing reunited Berlin as the sixteenth state. Soviet occupation forces withdrew entirely from German territory by 1994.

Cambodian Independence from France

Cambodia ended ninety years of French colonial protectorate rule in 1953 to establish full national sovereignty.

Who
Kingdom of Cambodia
What
Secured independence from France
Where
Phnom Penh, Cambodia
When
9 November 1953
What happened

In 1863, King Norodom sought French protection, establishing a protectorate that integrated Cambodia into French Indochina. In 1867, Norodom signed a treaty with France that ceded control of Battambang and Siem Reap provinces to Siam, though both territories were eventually restored to Cambodian jurisdiction under a 1906 treaty between France and Siam.

Imperial Japanese forces occupied Cambodia during World War II, but French colonial authority was restored following the end of the war. After ninety years under French administration, Cambodia formally secured its total independence in 1953, establishing Phnom Penh as the capital of the sovereign kingdom across its 181,035 square kilometers of territory.

What changed after

Following independence in 1953, sovereign Cambodian rule was re-established over its low-lying plains and the Mekong river basin. However, spillover from the Vietnam War during the 1960s destabilized the kingdom, leading to internal conflict and the eventual takeover by the Khmer Rouge regime in 1975.

Peaceful democratic governance was later restored under United Nations peacekeeping supervision, leading to a new constitution and restored monarchy in 1993.
