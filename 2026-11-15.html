1884 · Berlin, Germany

Berlin Conference of 1884–1885

Fourteen nations convened in Berlin to set rules for European imperial occupation and free trade across the African continent.

Adalbert von Roessler — Public domain · source
Who
Fourteen European powers and the United States
What
Regulated European colonisation and trade in Africa
Where
No. 77 Wilhelmstrasse, Berlin, Germany
When
15 November 1884
What happened

On 15 November 1884, representatives from fourteen countries gathered at No. 77 (now No. 92) Wilhelmstrasse in Berlin. German Chancellor Otto von Bismarck organised the assembly following a request from King Leopold II of Belgium. The delegates sought to establish international guidelines for colonial claims and economic activity across Africa, particularly within resource-rich areas such as the Congo Basin.

During the proceedings, delegates established rules for recognizing European effective occupation of coastal territories. Economic interests centered on expanding European industrial access to natural resources, including rubber, minerals, ivory, and cotton, while securing markets for manufactured goods. Six participating countries—Austria-Hungary, Russia, Denmark, the Netherlands, Sweden-Norway, and the United States—concluded the negotiations without acquiring any territory. The conference adjourned before concluding on 26 February 1885 with the signing of the General Act of Berlin.

What changed after

The signing of the General Act established a common legal framework for European powers to declare effective occupation along African coasts. Economic rivalries between Britain and France intensified as nations rushed to claim resource-bearing territories under the new guidelines.

A 2024 study demonstrated that while the meeting formalised imperial ambitions, its immediate boundary agreements were limited to the Congo region. Most colonial borders across the African continent were finalized over two decades after the conference ended.

1933 Siamese General Election

Voters across Siam participated in the country's inaugural legislative election to fill half of the House of Representatives.

Who
Voters and sub-district representatives in Siam
What
Held indirect elections for parliament
Where
Siam
When
15 November 1933
What happened

Siam conducted its first general election on an indirect basis between 10 October and 15 November 1933. Eligible citizens voted for sub-district representatives across the country. On 16 November, these elected local representatives selected 78 members for the House of Representatives.

Because no political parties existed in Siam at the time, all candidates contested the election as independents. The election achieved a voter turnout rate of 41 percent. King Prajadhipok appointed the remaining 78 seats of the 156-member legislature directly.

What changed after

The election populated the 156-seat House of Representatives with 78 elected independents alongside 78 royal appointees. This established Siam's first parliamentary body comprising both elected and appointed members.

Jimmy Choo

Born on this day, 1948

Malaysian-born footwear designer Jimmy Choo built an international fashion house renowned for handcrafted luxury women's shoes.

What they did

Jimmy Choo Yeang Keat was born in 1948 in Penang into a Hakka Chinese family of shoemakers. His father taught him shoemaking, and Choo crafted his first pair of shoes, a pair of slippers, at age 11. Between 1982 and 1984, he studied at Cordwainers Technical College in London. After graduating, he worked at two design companies over nine years before launching his own footwear business in 1986, opening a shop on Kingsland Road in London.

Choo gained international attention after London Fashion Week in 1988 led to an eight-page spread in Vogue magazine. Princess Diana became a patron in the early 1990s. In 1996, Choo co-founded Jimmy Choo Ltd. alongside British Vogue accessories editor Tamara Mellon. He sold his 50 percent stake in the enterprise for £10 million in April 2001 to focus on his couture footwear line.

What still stands

Choo holds the title of Commander of the Order of Meritorious Service (PJN Datuk), awarded by Malaysia in 2013, as well as an honorary doctorate in art from De Montfort University. He continues to operate the Jimmy Choo couture line produced under license from Jimmy Choo Ltd.
