1939 · Poland

Invasion of Poland and Start of World War II in Europe

German forces invaded Poland on 1 September 1939, initiating combat across Europe that ultimately resulted in tens of millions of deaths.

What happened

In 1938, German dictator Adolf Hitler annexed Austria and part of Czechoslovakia to gain living space for the German population. Germany was supported by Benito Mussolini's Italy and subsequently signed a non-aggression pact with Joseph Stalin's Soviet Union.

On 1 September 1939, Germany launched an invasion of Poland. Days later, Poland's allies, France and the United Kingdom, declared war on Germany. Soviet forces subsequently invaded eastern Poland, resulting in the partition of Polish territory between German and Soviet control.

What changed after

Following the invasion of Poland, Germany expanded its offensives into Norway, Denmark, France, Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg in 1940. The Allied coalition of the United Kingdom, Soviet Union, United States, and France engaged Axis forces across European fronts until Germany surrendered on 8 May 1945.

After the war, the Allies occupied Europe and held the Nuremberg trials to prosecute German military leaders. Western Europe established capitalist governments, while Eastern Europe became communist under Soviet influence.

In 1959, an attempted assassination targeting King Norodom Sihanouk of Cambodia using a parcel bomb sent through the mail failed.

What happened

A letter bomb or parcel bomb is an explosive device sent through postal services designed to explode upon opening to maim or kill the recipient. Such devices have been used in targeted political assassinations and attacks.

In 1959, South Vietnamese chief adviser Ngô Đình Nhu dispatched a parcel bomb targeting King Norodom Sihanouk of Cambodia. The mail bomb failed to kill the Cambodian monarch.

What changed after

Investigative agencies in various countries maintain oversight of postal security to detect and interdict letter bombs. Under Article 53(a) of the European Patent Convention, explosive mail devices are excluded from patentability on grounds of public order and morality.

Independence of the Federation of Malaya

The Federation of Malaya achieved full independence from the United Kingdom on 31 August 1957 after earlier structural reorganizations.

What happened

During World War II, British Malaya was occupied by the Empire of Japan for three years. Following the end of Japanese occupation, the territory was unified as the Malayan Union in 1946 before being restructured into the Federation of Malaya in 1948.

On 31 August 1957, the Federation of Malaya officially achieved its independence from the United Kingdom.

What changed after

On 16 September 1963, independent Malaya joined with North Borneo, Sarawak, and Singapore to establish Malaysia. In August 1965, Singapore was separated from the federation and became an independent country.

Malaysia established a federal constitutional monarchy comprising 13 states and three federal territories. The country designated Kuala Lumpur as its legislative capital and Putrajaya as its federal administrative and judicial center.
