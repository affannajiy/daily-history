Proclamation of the People's Republic of China

Mao Zedong declared the founding of the People's Republic of China in Beijing after the Chinese Communist Party defeated Nationalist forces.

What happened

On September 21, 1949, Chairman Mao Zedong announced the establishment of the People's Republic of China during a speech at the first plenary session of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference. Discussions held prior to the public declaration centered on the state's name; Mao initially proposed retaining the name Republic of China, but overseas Chinese entrepreneur-activist Tan Kah Kee strongly objected to using the old name. The committee ultimately adopted Zhang Xiruo's suggestion to use the name People's Republic of China.

At 15:00 Beijing Time on October 1, 1949, Mao stood atop Tiananmen Gate in Beijing and proclaimed to the nation that the Central People's Government of the People's Republic of China was established. "March of the Volunteers" was played as the national anthem, and the Five-starred Red Flag was hoisted for the first time while a 21-gun salute fired in the distance. The ceremony concluded with the first public military parade of the People's Liberation Army.

What changed after

The Central People's Government assumed political authority over mainland China under the Chinese Communist Party. "March of the Volunteers" became the national anthem, and the Five-starred Red Flag was instituted as the new national flag.

By December 1949, the Kuomintang-led Nationalist government retreated offshore to Taiwan, Hainan, and their surrounding islands, ending Nationalist governance on the Chinese mainland.
