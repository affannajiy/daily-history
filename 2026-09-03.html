1783 · Paris, France

Signing of the Treaty of Paris

Great Britain recognized the independence of the United States and ceded vast territories east of the Mississippi River in a formal peace treaty.

iDK — Public domain · source
Who
Representatives of Great Britain and the United States
What
Signed treaty ending the Revolutionary War
Where
Hôtel d'York, Paris, France
When
September 3, 1783
What happened

Peace negotiations began in Paris in April 1782 following the fall of British Prime Minister Lord North's government on March 20. American delegates Benjamin Franklin, John Jay, Henry Laurens, and John Adams negotiated with British representatives David Hartley and Richard Oswald. French Foreign Minister Vergennes proposed limiting the United States to the area east of the Appalachian Mountains, but John Jay opened direct negotiations with British Prime Minister Lord Shelburne to secure broader terms.

Timeline

January 14, 1784 The Congress of the Confederation ratifies the treaty in Annapolis, Maryland.
April 9, 1784 Great Britain ratifies the treaty prior to the exchange of ratified copies on May 12.

What changed after

Great Britain recognized the Thirteen Colonies as independent states and established borders extending to the Mississippi River in the west, Florida in the south, and present-day Canada in the north. Under separate agreements forming the Peace of Paris, Britain ceded East Florida, West Florida, and Menorca to Spain, while France received Tobago and Senegal.

Southeast Asia
1997 · Phnom Penh, Cambodia

Vietnam Airlines Flight 815 crash

A Vietnam Airlines Tupolev Tu-134 crashed during heavy rain short of the Phnom Penh runway, leaving a single survivor.

Who
Captain Pham Van Tieu and Flight 815 crew
What
Crashed Tupolev airliner during approach
Where
Pochentong International Airport, Phnom Penh, Cambodia
When
September 3, 1997, at 1:40 pm
What happened

Vietnam Airlines Flight 815 departed Ho Chi Minh City around 1:00 pm on September 3, 1997, carrying 66 passengers and crew to Phnom Penh. Approaching Pochentong International Airport in heavy rain, the Tupolev Tu-134B-3 descended to 10,000 feet using a non-directional beacon because the airport's VOR/DME equipment had been looted two months earlier. The control tower granted permission to drop to 2,000 feet and requested the pilot switch to an western approach on runway 5 due to changing winds.

Timeline
July 1997 VOR/DME navigation equipment at Pochentong International Airport is looted.
September 3, 1997, 1:00 pm Flight 815 departs Ho Chi Minh City for Phnom Penh.
September 3, 1997, 1:40 pm Flight 815 strikes a tree and crashes short of the runway.
Key figures
Pham Van Tieu
Flight captain
Continued the landing approach despite failing to spot the runway and ignoring requests to pull up.
Hoang Van Dinh
First officer
Twice requested the captain abort the landing as the aircraft descended below safe altitudes.
Chanayuth Nim-anong
Passenger
A 14-month-old boy from Thailand who survived as the sole survivor of the crash.
What changed after

An investigation by Cambodia's Aircraft Accident Investigation Committee determined that pilot error caused the crash. Local villagers recovered the aircraft's three flight recorders from the crash site and sold them back to the Cambodian government for rewards of $10, $200, and $1,500.
