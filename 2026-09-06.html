Global Headline
1914 · Marne River valley, France

First Battle of the Marne

Allied forces mounted a counter-offensive along a 230-kilometre frontline to stop the Imperial German Army's swift advance on Paris.

German Army — Public domain · source
Who
Franco-British forces against Imperial German Army
What
Fought the First Battle of the Marne
Where
Between Paris and Verdun, France
When
September 5 to 12, 1914
What happened

In August 1914, the Imperial German Army invaded France with plans to capture Paris within 40 days, driving French and British forces back over 250 km during the Great Retreat. The Allied forces halted near the Marne River valley, 40 km from Paris. Field Marshal John French considered retreating to English Channel ports for evacuation, but French commander Joseph Joffre maintained army order, drawing reinforcements from his eastern flank and integrating military reserve units to outnumber exhausted German troops.

On September 3, military governor of Paris Joseph Gallieni spotted an exposed German right flank. Joffre ordered a counter-offensive on September 4, launching engagement across a 230 km front between Paris and Verdun. By September 9, Allied attacks threatened the German 1st and 2nd Armies with encirclement, forcing a retreat 65 km north to the Aisne River.

What changed after

The German retreat of 65 km to the Aisne River ended Germany's strategy to defeat France quickly. Both armies dug in along the heights north of the river, fighting the First Battle of the Aisne.

Following the battle, both sides initiated flank-enveloping movements northward in the Race to the Sea. These actions culminated in the First Battle of Ypres and established a four-year stalemate of trench warfare across the Western Front.

Southeast Asia
2009 · Zamboanga Peninsula, Philippines

Sinking of SuperFerry 9

A Filipino passenger ferry capsized off the Zamboanga Peninsula while carrying 971 people, resulting in a rescue effort that saved all but ten aboard.

Who
Aboitiz Transport System Corp and rescue responders
What
Ferry SuperFerry 9 listing and sinking
Where
Off southwest coast of Zamboanga Peninsula
When
September 6, 2009, approximately 9:00 a.m.
What happened

At 8:45 a.m. on September 5, 2009, SuperFerry 9 departed General Santos for Iloilo City carrying 971 passengers and crew. Between 3:00 and 4:00 a.m. on September 6, Captain Jose Yap transmitted a distress call stating the vessel was listing to starboard. Passengers reported loud heavy crashing noises, indicating cargo containers may have shifted in the hold and damaged the hull. Captain Yap ordered passengers to abandon ship an hour after the distress signal.

At 5:20 a.m., the cargo vessel MV Myriad arrived at the scene, taking off half the passengers from life rafts within an hour. SuperFerry 9 capsized and sank at roughly 9:00 a.m., approximately 150 km from Zamboanga City. Disaster response officials accounted for 961 survivors alongside 10 fatalities.

What changed after

On September 7, 2009, the Maritime Industry Authority issued a suspension order grounding the entire vessel fleet of Aboitiz Transport System Corp, though the company successfully contested the whole-of-fleet grounding.

A containment vessel was dispatched to the sinking site after a military aircraft spotted an oil slick above the wreck, which rested on the seabed 18 km offshore.

Tanah Melayu / Malaysia
1997 · Lambir Hills National Park, Malaysia

Crash of Royal Brunei Airlines Flight 839

A charter flight operated on behalf of Royal Brunei Airlines crashed into a slope during approach to Miri Airport, killing everyone aboard.

Who
Flight crew of operator Merpati Intan
What
Dornier 228 aircraft crashed on approach
Where
Lambir Hills National Park, Sarawak, Malaysia
When
September 6, 1997, at 19:42 local time
What happened

Royal Brunei Airlines Flight 839, a short-haul charter flight operated by Merpati Intan using a Dornier 228 aircraft registered as 9M-MIA, departed Brunei International Airport at 19:13 local time on September 6, 1997. The aircraft was bound for Miri Airport in Sarawak, Malaysia, carrying two Malaysian crew members and eight passengers, including two Japanese tourists, one Sri Lankan, one Singaporean, and four Malaysians.

Air traffic control cleared the flight for final approach to Runway 2 at Miri Airport, but the crew did not radio back. At 19:42 local time, the aircraft crashed into a slope at an altitude of 500 metres in Lambir Hills National Park. Search teams located the wreckage at 07:10 the following morning, confirming all ten occupants were killed.

What changed after

The incident marked the second aviation crash in Miri following a September 1991 accident that killed 14 people, and stood as the sixth aircraft accident in Malaysia during 1997.

Investigators were unable to retrieve a cockpit voice recorder from the crash site in Lambir Hills National Park.
