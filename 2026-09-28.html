1066 · England

Landing of William the Conqueror in England

Duke William of Normandy crossed the English Channel with a large fleet to claim the English crown from Harold Godwinson.

Myrabella — Public domain · source
Who
William, Duke of Normandy

Where
England
When
September 1066
What happened

Following the death of King Edward the Confessor in January 1066, Earl Harold Godwinson took the English throne. William, Duke of Normandy, disputed Harold's ascension, asserting that Edward had earlier promised the throne to him and that Harold had sworn to support his claim. In response, William built a large fleet and assembled a Franco-Norman army to enforce his claim by force.

In September 1066, William landed his forces in England. He engaged the Anglo-Saxon army led by Harold Godwinson at the Battle of Hastings on 14 October 1066, where Harold was killed and his army was defeated. William marched to London and was crowned King of England on Christmas Day, 1066.

What changed after

William's coronation led to the suppression of English revolts and the settlement of a new Norman nobility across English lands. Castle construction expanded throughout England, and the composition of the English clergy altered under Norman administration.

In 1086, William ordered the survey known as the Domesday Book to record all land-holdings in England. Upon his death in September 1087, his territory was divided, with Normandy passing to his eldest son Robert Curthose and England going to his second surviving son William Rufus.

1901 · Balangiga, Philippines

Balangiga Encounter

Filipino forces launched a surprise attack on an occupying US Infantry company at Balangiga on the island of Samar.

Who
Filipino guerrillas and town residents
What
Attacked Company C, 9th US Infantry
Where
Balangiga, Samar, Philippines
When
September 28, 1901
What happened

On August 11, 1901, Company C of the US 9th Infantry Regiment arrived in Balangiga to close its port and prevent supplies from reaching Filipino forces under Captain General Vicente Lukban in the interior of Samar. Town mayor Pedro Abayan had previously written to Lukban on May 30, 1901, stating that the town would feign compliance with the Americans until an opportunity arose to rise against them.

On September 28, 1901, police chief Valeriano Abanador led residents and guerrillas in a surprise attack planned by Captain Eugenio Daza against the stationed American troops. The operation resulted in 54 American soldiers killed.

What changed after

In response to the attack, American General Jacob H. Smith ordered pacification actions across the island of Samar. These retaliatory operations resulted in an estimated 2,000 Filipino civilians killed and over 200 homes burned.
