Sputnik 1 launch

The Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite, igniting the space race and reshaping global politics.

What happened

On 4 October 1957 the Soviet Union placed Sputnik 1 into an elliptical low‑Earth orbit from Site No.1/5 at the 5th Tyuratam range in the Kazakh SSR. The polished metal sphere measured 58 cm in diameter, carried four external radio antennas and transmitted on 20.005 and 40.002 MHz, completing each orbit in 96.20 minutes while travelling at about 8 km/s. Its radio pulses were easily detected worldwide, and its orbital inclination of 65° allowed it to pass over virtually the entire inhabited Earth.

The satellite broadcast continuously for three weeks until its silver‑zinc batteries were exhausted on 26 October 1957. Aerodynamic drag then caused it to decay, and after completing 1,440 orbits and travelling roughly 70,000,000 km it re‑entered the atmosphere on 4 January 1958, burning up and ending the mission.

What changed after

The launch triggered the American Sputnik crisis, prompting the United States to accelerate its own space program and eventually establish NASA. The event also marked the beginning of the Cold War space race, leading both superpowers to invest heavily in satellite and missile technology. The Soviet Union’s success demonstrated the feasibility of orbital flight, influencing subsequent missions such as the launch of the first human‑rated spacecraft and the development of intercontinental ballistic missiles.

What happened

Omar Ali Saifuddien III, born on 23 September 1914, succeeded Sultan Ahmad Tajuddin on 6 June 1950 and was crowned on 31 May 1951. During his reign he oversaw the introduction of the 1959 Constitution, the establishment of modern administrative structures, and the development of key national institutions. In 1967 he voluntarily abdicated the throne in favour of his son, ending a seventeen‑year reign and ushering in a new era of leadership for Brunei.
