1519 · Tenochtitlán, Aztec Empire

Hernán Cortés Enters Tenochtitlán

Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés and his forces entered the Aztec capital of Tenochtitlan during initial contact with emperor Moctezuma II.

What happened

During the reign of Moctezuma Xocoyotzin, the ninth emperor of the Aztec Empire who came to power in 1502 or 1503, Europeans made their first contact with Mesoamerican civilizations. Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés led his men into the Aztec empire during the initial stages of the Spanish conquest.

Moctezuma II, who had previously served as a high-ranking military commander holding the rank of tlacatecuhtli and main priest of the temple of Huitzilopochtli, received the Spanish forces at Tenochtitlan. Cortés and his men subsequently seized the capital, leading to the death of Moctezuma on 29 June 1520.

What changed after

Following the death of Moctezuma II during the conquest of Tenochtitlan, two successor rulers held short-lived reigns before the Aztec Empire collapsed. The Spanish conquest halted the territorial expansion that had reached Xoconosco in Chiapas and the Isthmus of Tehuantepec.

Typhoon Haiyan Strikes the Visayas

A severe tropical cyclone struck the central Philippine archipelago of Visayas, resulting in thousands of fatalities and billions in damage.

What happened

Typhoon Haiyan struck the Visayas region in the central Philippines as one of the strongest tropical cyclones ever recorded. The storm swept through the major islands of the region, including Panay, Negros, Cebu, Bohol, Leyte, and Samar.

The cyclone killed at least 6,340 people across the affected territory, while over 1,000 individuals remained missing. The disaster caused $2.86 billion in damages in 2013 USD across the island group.

What changed after

The storm impacted four administrative regions across the Visayas, including Eastern Visayas, Central Visayas, and Western Visayas. Reconstruction efforts proceeded across major islands including Leyte, Samar, and Cebu to rebuild infrastructure.

Mohd Faiz Subri

Born on this day, 1987

Malaysian footballer Mohd Faiz Subri became the first Asian recipient of the FIFA Puskás Award after scoring a viral free kick in 2016.

What they did

Mohd Faiz bin Subri began his football career with the Kedah youth team in 2006 before joining FAM League club Tambun Tulang in 2009, where he scored 6 goals in 14 league appearances. He subsequently played for Malaysia Super League clubs Perlis FA and T-Team FC before signing a one-year contract with Kelantan FA in 2013. During his stint with Kelantan, he scored against Vietnamese club SHB Đà Nẵng in the AFC Cup and won the 2013 Malaysia FA Cup.

After playing for Terengganu FA in 2014, Faiz moved to Penang FA, helping the club earn second place in the 2015 Malaysia Premier League and promotion to the Malaysia Super League. On 16 February 2016, while playing for Penang against Pahang FA at the City Stadium in George Town, he scored a curling free kick in a 4–1 victory. The goal led to his nomination and win of the 2016 FIFA Puskás Award in 2017, making him the first Asian player to receive the honour.
