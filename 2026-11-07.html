Global Headline
1917 · Petrograd, Russia

October Revolution

Bolshevik insurgents launched a coordinated uprising in Petrograd, seizing government buildings and taking control of the Russian capital.

Pyotr Novitsky — Public domain · source
Who
Bolshevik Red Guards led by Vladimir Lenin
What
Captured the Winter Palace and government buildings
Where
Petrograd, Russia
When
7 November 1917 [O.S. 25 October 1917]
What happened

In autumn 1917, Russia faced severe economic disruption, with industrial output falling by over 36% compared to 1914 levels and national debt reaching 50 billion roubles. The Russian Provisional Government under Alexander Kerensky remained deeply unpopular for continuing participation in World War I. On 10 [N.S. 23] October 1917, the Petrograd Soviet, headed by Leon Trotsky, voted to support an armed insurrection against Kerensky's administration. When government forces attempted to close newspapers and isolate Petrograd on 24 October [N.S. 6 November], Bolshevik Red Guards under the Military-Revolutionary Committee initiated targeted actions across the capital.

A fleet of Bolshevik sailors entered the Petrograd harbor on 25 October [N.S. 7 November] as tens of thousands of soldiers rose in support. Red Guard units systematically occupied key administrative buildings throughout the city with minimal casualties. In the early morning of 26 October [N.S. 8 November], Bolshevik forces stormed and captured the Winter Palace, ending the authority of the Provisional Government.

What changed after

Armed resistance to Bolshevik control triggered the Russian Civil War, which lasted until late 1922 and resulted in the establishment of the Soviet Union. The new administration established October Revolution Day as an official Soviet public holiday to mark the state's origin.

Southeast Asia
1426 · Đông Quan, Đại Việt

Battle of Tốt Động – Chúc Động

Lam Sơn rebels deployed hand cannons and staged a devastating ambush in the Red River Delta to rout an advancing Ming army.

Who
Lam Sơn rebels under Lê Lợi
What
Ambushed and routed the Ming army
Where
Tốt Động and Chúc Động, Đại Việt
When
November 7, 1426
What happened

In November 1426, Lam Sơn rebel detachments under General Lý Triện reached the vicinity of Đông Quan. After defeating Ming forces in preliminary engagements near Ninh Kiều and the Nhân Mục bridge—causing over 3,000 Ming casualties—rebel commanders regrouped. Ming General Wang Tong led three columns out of Đông Quan citadel to crush the uprising. In early skirmishes, Lý Triện feigned a retreat past the Tam La bridge, trapping Ming troops in mud and rice paddies where 1,000 were killed and 500 captured.

Following intelligence gathered from captured Ming spies, commanders Đinh Lễ and Lý Triện learned that Wang Tong intended to cross the river and flank the rebel position. The Vietnamese forces maintained absolute silence until Wang Tong's troops entered an ambush zone near the Yên Duyệt River. Lam Sơn soldiers armed with hand cannons attacked the advancing columns between the villages of Tốt Động and Chúc Động. Vietnamese sources recorded 50,000 Ming soldiers killed and 10,000 captured, while Wang Tong suffered wounds and retreated to Đông Quan.

What changed after

Vietnamese forces captured Ming weapons, horses, and military supplies before encircling Wang Tong inside the citadel of Đông Quan four days after the battle. The defeat broke Ming offensive capabilities in the Red River Delta, leading to the full restoration of Vietnamese independence under Lê Lợi in 1428.
