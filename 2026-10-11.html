1899 · South Africa

Outbreak of the Second Boer War

British forces and Boer republics entered open warfare in South Africa after disputes over gold field political rights broke down negotiations.

South Africa
When
October 11, 1899
What happened

Tensions mounted following the 1884 discovery of gold in the Witwatersrand gold fields within the South African Republic. Foreign workers, primarily British subjects from the Cape Colony known as Uitlanders, were denied voting rights until achieving 14 years of residence. Protests to British colonial authorities led to the Bloemfontein Conference in June 1899, but negotiations failed to reach an agreement. In response, the British government resolved to dispatch 10,000 troops to the region, prompting the outbreak of armed conflict in October 1899.

Boer forces mounted offensive strikes into British-controlled territory in Natal and the Cape Colony, laying siege to garrisons at Ladysmith, Mafeking, and Kimberley. The Boers secured early victories at Stormberg, Magersfontein, Colenso, and Spion Kop. British command later replaced General Redvers Buller with Lord Roberts and Lord Kitchener, who deployed an expeditionary force of 180,000 troops to relieve the besieged cities and occupy the Boer capitals.

What changed after

The South African Republic and Orange Free State were annexed by the British Empire in 1900 and converted into the Transvaal and Orange River colonies. Under the Treaty of Vereeniging signed in May 1902, Boer fighters surrendered their weapons in exchange for release from internment camps.

In 1910, the Transvaal and Orange River colonies merged with the Natal and Cape colonies to form the Union of South Africa. British counterinsurgency operations forcibly interned over 100,000 Boer civilians, leading to 26,000 deaths from disease and starvation, alongside 20,000 native African deaths in separate camps.

French Withdrawal from North Vietnam

French military forces withdrew from North Vietnam following ceasefire agreements reached at the multi-nation Geneva Conference.

Where

When
October 1954
What happened

Hostilities in the First Indochina War culminated following the defeat of French forces at the Battle of Dien Bien Phu. International diplomats subsequently convened at the Palace of Nations in Geneva, Switzerland, from 26 April to 21 July 1954 to establish terms ending the war. The conference involved representatives from France, the Democratic Republic of Vietnam, the State of Vietnam, the Soviet Union, the United States, the United Kingdom, China, Laos, and Cambodia.

The proceedings concluded with three binding ceasefire agreements that partitioned Vietnam at the 17th parallel. Democratic Republic of Vietnam troops and personnel regrouped to the northern zone, while State of Vietnam forces gathered in the south. In accordance with these Geneva Accords, French troops completed their evacuation from North Vietnam, permitting the Viet Minh administration to establish civil and military authority across the northern territory.

What changed after

Vietnam was partitioned at the 17th parallel into two separate military regrouping zones. The communist Democratic Republic of Vietnam established control over the northern zone, while the State of Vietnam governed the territory south of the parallel.

The ceasefire agreements restricted Pathet Lao combatants to two northern provinces in Laos and disbanded Khmer Issarak forces in Cambodia. An International Control Commission was created for international supervision, although delegates from the United States and the State of Vietnam declined to sign the Final Declaration.
