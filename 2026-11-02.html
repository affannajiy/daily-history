1917 · London, United Kingdom

Balfour Declaration

British Foreign Secretary Arthur Balfour issued a public statement supporting a Jewish national home in Ottoman-controlled Palestine.

United Kingdom Government signed by Arthur Balfour — Public domain · source
Who
British Foreign Secretary Arthur Balfour
What
Issued statement supporting a Jewish national home
Where
London, United Kingdom
When
2 November 1917
What happened

In November 1914, Britain declared war on the Ottoman Empire and began evaluating the future of Palestine. Zionist War Cabinet member Herbert Samuel circulated a memorandum proposing support for Zionist ambitions, leading Prime Minister H. H. Asquith to establish a committee on Ottoman policy in April 1915. After David Lloyd George replaced Asquith in December 1916, negotiations opened on 7 February 1917 between Sir Mark Sykes and Zionist leaders. On 19 June, Foreign Secretary Arthur Balfour asked Lord Rothschild and Chaim Weizmann to draft a formal declaration.

Discussions continued through September and October 1917, balancing Zionist and anti-Zionist Jewish input without local Palestinian Arab representation. On 31 October 1917, following the Battle of Beersheba, the British Cabinet authorised the release of the final statement, aiming to secure Jewish propaganda support for the Allied war effort. On 2 November 1917, Balfour sent the completed letter to Rothschild for transmission to the Zionist Federation, with the text appearing in the press on 9 November 1917.

What changed after

The text was incorporated into the British Mandate for Palestine, serving as its founding document. In 1939, the British government acknowledged that the local population's interests should have been taken into account, and in 2018 it recognized that the declaration should have protected Palestinian Arabs' political rights. The policy indirectly led to the emergence of the State of Israel.

1949 · The Hague, Netherlands

Dutch–Indonesian Round Table Conference

The Netherlands agreed to transfer sovereignty to the United States of Indonesia following negotiations in The Hague.

Who
Kingdom of the Netherlands and Indonesian representatives
What
Agreed to transfer sovereignty to Indonesia
Where
The Hague, Netherlands
When
2 November 1949
What happened

Following nationalist leader Sukarno's declaration of Indonesian independence on 17 August 1945, Dutch attempts to regain control triggered the Indonesian National Revolution. On 28 January 1949, United Nations Security Council Resolution 67 demanded an end to Dutch military operations and the restoration of the republican government. Indonesian leaders returned from exile on Bangka Island to Yogyakarta on 6 July 1949, holding Inter-Indonesian Conferences from 31 July to 2 August to establish principles for a federal constitution.

Negotiations opened in The Hague on 23 August 1949 between the Kingdom of the Netherlands, the Republic of Indonesia, and the Federal Consultative Assembly, assisted by the United Nations Commission for Indonesia. Delegates deadlocked over colonial administration debt and the political status of Western New Guinea. On 24 October, Indonesia agreed to assume ƒ4.5 billion in debt, and on 1 November, a compromise deferred Western New Guinea's status to future negotiations, leading to the conference's official closure on 2 November 1949.

What changed after

The conference produced a Charter of Transfer of Sovereignty that created the United States of Indonesia and arranged the withdrawal of Dutch troops. A consultative Dutch–Indonesian Union was formed under the symbolic headship of the Dutch Queen, while the status of Western New Guinea was slated for resolution within one year.
