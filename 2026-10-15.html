1582 · Rome, Papal States

Adoption of the Gregorian calendar

Pope Gregory XIII instituted a papal bull to correct calendar drift caused by the inaccurate Julian calculation of Easter.

Where

When

What happened

Pope Gregory XIII issued the Papal bull Inter gravissimas to correct an error in the Julian Calendar that caused an erroneous calculation of the date of Easter. The Julian system was based on a year of 365.25 days, which was slightly too long compared to the actual 365.2422 days, causing the calendar to drift out of alignment with Earth's orbit. Scientific advisors calculated that 10 excess leap days had accumulated since the First Council of Nicaea in AD 325, prompting a ruling that day numbering must jump by 10 days to restore alignment.

Catholic polities including Spain, Portugal, Poland–Lithuania, and Italian principalities implemented the change immediately. Thursday, October 4, 1582, was followed directly by Friday, October 15, 1582, omitting 10 dates. Other regions followed subsequently, with France adopting the new calendar in December 1582, while Protestant and Eastern Orthodox polities initially resisted the Catholic reform, resulting in differing holiday and Easter dates across denominations.

What changed after

The civil calendar diverged from ecclesiastical calendars as Protestant and Eastern Orthodox polities initially rejected the papal decree, causing Christian denominations to celebrate Easter and holidays on different dates. Protestant estates eventually adopted an improved calendar in 1700 without the lunar portion, before transitioning fully to Gregorian Easter calculations later in 1774.

2013 · Bohol, Philippines

2013 Bohol earthquake

A magnitude 7.2 earthquake struck the Central Visayas region of the Philippines, destroying thousands of structures and causing hundreds of deaths.

What happened

A magnitude 7.2 earthquake struck the island province of Bohol in Central Visayas, Philippines, with its epicenter near Sagbayan and a depth of focus of 12 kilometres. The tremor affected the entire Central Visayas region, hitting Bohol and Cebu particularly hard, and was felt as far away as Masbate island and Cotabato provinces in Mindanao. Official reports documented 222 deaths, 8 missing persons, and 976 injuries, alongside more than 73,000 damaged structures of which over 14,500 were completely destroyed.

Geological analysis revealed the earthquake was generated by the previously unmapped North Bohol Fault, producing surface ruptures with vertical displacements reaching up to 5 metres. Thousands of aftershocks rattled Bohol Island and Cebu over the following months, totaling 4,026 recorded tremors by December 6, 2013. Three weeks after the disaster, Super Typhoon Haiyan struck the broader region, forcing approximately 40,000 Bohol residents living in temporary shelters back into evacuation centers.
