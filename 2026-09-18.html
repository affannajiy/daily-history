1947 · Washington, D.C., United States

Enactment of the National Security Act of 1947

The United States implemented sweeping administrative changes on September 18, 1947, by officially establishing unified defense and intelligence organizations.

U.S. Government — Public domain · source
Who
United States Federal Government
What
Restructured military and intelligence agencies
Where
Washington, D.C., United States
When
September 18, 1947
What happened

Efforts to unify the United States military began in 1944 following World War II, driven by command friction between the Army and Navy in the Pacific and intelligence failures identified after the attack on Pearl Harbor. On February 26, 1947, President Harry S. Truman proposed reorganization legislation to Congress. Introduced in the House and Senate under the sponsorship of Senator Chan Gurney, chairman of the Senate Committee on Armed Services, the bill passed both chambers by voice vote in July 1947. President Truman signed the legislation into law on July 26, 1947, aboard the presidential aircraft Sacred Cow.

The majority of the act's provisions took effect on September 18, 1947, one day after the Senate confirmed James Forrestal as the first secretary of defense. The legislation merged the Department of the Navy and the Department of the Army—formerly the Department of War—alongside the newly formed Department of the Air Force into the National Military Establishment. The act separated the Army Air Forces into the independent United States Air Force while preserving the Marine Corps under the Department of the Navy.

What changed after

The National Security Act created the National Security Council to coordinate defense policy and established the Central Intelligence Agency under the direction of the director of central intelligence. The legislation replaced the wartime voluntary cooperation among armed service branches with a centralized National Military Establishment headed by the secretary of defense.

End of the 8888 Uprising

A bloody military coup ended months of nationwide pro-democracy demonstrations across Myanmar on September 18, 1988.

Who
State Law and Order Restoration Council
What
Suppressed nationwide protests through a military coup
Where
Rangoon, Burma
When
September 18, 1988
What happened

General Ne Win had ruled Burma as a single-party state under the Burma Socialist Programme Party since 1962. Economic decline, military spending that reached 40% of the national budget, and currency demonetisations in November 1985 and September 1987 eliminated millions of citizens' savings. In late 1987, protests broke out led by students from the Rangoon Institute of Technology and Rangoon Arts and Sciences University.

On August 8, 1988, students organized nationwide marches that drew hundreds of thousands of monks, civil servants, medical workers, and citizens into the streets. Troops fired into crowds on Ne Win's orders, killing demonstrators on the Red Bridge in Rangoon. On September 18, 1988, General Saw Maung led a bloody military coup that established the State Law and Order Restoration Council, forcibly dispersing the demonstrations and ending the uprising.

What changed after

The State Law and Order Restoration Council assumed direct control of Burma, replacing the Burma Socialist Programme Party while keeping military rule intact. The junta arrested over 300 supporters of the opposition National League for Democracy, including 200 party workers and 15 members of parliament, and placed Aung San Suu Kyi under house arrest after refusing to recognize her party's 81% victory in the 1990 election.
