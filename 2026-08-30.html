70 · Jerusalem, Judaea

Siege of Jerusalem

Roman forces led by Titus captured Jerusalem and destroyed the Second Temple, concluding the siege during the First Jewish–Roman War.

Marie-Lan Nguyen — Public domain · source

What happened

During the First Jewish–Roman War, Titus served as a military commander under his father, Vespasian. The campaign briefly halted following the death of Emperor Nero in AD 68, which launched Vespasian's bid for imperial power. After Vespasian was declared Emperor on July 1, 69, Titus was left in charge of ending the Jewish rebellion in Judaea.

In AD 70, Titus besieged Jerusalem, successfully capturing the city and destroying both Jerusalem and the Second Temple. Following the victory, Titus returned to Rome, where he received a triumph and was later commemorated by the Arch of Titus.

What changed after

Following the destruction of Jerusalem and the Second Temple, Titus was awarded a triumph in Rome. The Roman Senate later deified Titus after his death, and the Arch of Titus was erected in Rome, where it remains standing today.

Battle of San Juan del Monte

Spanish Governor-General Ramón Blanco declared martial law over eight provinces following a major engagement between Katipunan revolutionaries and Spanish colonial forces.

Who
Ramón Blanco y Erenas and Andrés Bonifacio
What
Declared martial law following a revolutionary battle
Where
San Juan del Monte, Philippines
When
August 30, 1896, 8:00 p.m.
What happened

On the evening of August 29, 1896, Andrés Bonifacio and Emilio Jacinto led Katipuneros toward El Polvorin, a Spanish powder magazine in San Juan del Monte defended by 30 men from the 70th Regiment and 35 artilleros. Bonifacio's forces were joined by 100 Katipunan members under Sancho Valenzuela coming from Santa Mesa.

In the early morning of August 30, Spanish reinforcements from the 70th "Magallanes" Regiment under General Bernardo Echaluce y Jauregui arrived. The Spanish forces inflicted heavy losses on Bonifacio's men, killing about 150 and capturing over 200, which forced Bonifacio to retreat toward the Pasig River. At 8:00 p.m. that evening, Governor-General Ramón Blanco y Erenas issued an executive order placing eight provinces under martial law.

What changed after

Governor-General Blanco placed Manila, Pampanga, Laguna, Cavite, Batangas, Bulacan, Nueva Ecija, and Tarlac under martial law. Fifty-seven revolutionaries were executed on August 31, 1896, followed by Sancho Valenzuela and three others on September 4, and thirteen revolutionaries in Cavite on September 12. President Corazon C. Aquino signed Executive Order 292 on July 25, 1987, establishing an annual public holiday on the last Sunday of August.

Michael Wong

Born on this day, 1970

Malaysian singer-songwriter Michael Wong achieved widespread popularity in the Mandopop scene through his solo recordings and compositions for other artists.

What they did

Michael Wong Kong Leong was born in Ipoh, Malaysia, and attended SMJK Sam Tet, where he sang in the church choir and wrote songs during secondary school. He moved to Kuala Lumpur to study computer engineering before pursuing audio engineering at the Ocean Institute of Audio Engineering. After entering up to 30 singing competitions in a single year, he formed a music duo with Victor Wong known as Michael & Victor, or Wu Yin Liang Pin, signing with Rock Records after submitting the song "The Palm".

The duo's debut album went platinum in Malaysia and Taiwan before the pair split in 2000. Wong then began a solo career, producing *Michael's First Album* and releasing *Ray of Light* in November 2002, which became the top-selling album in Taiwan. In January 2005, he released his third solo album, *Fairy Tale*, writing and composing its title track, which topped the Baidu 500 download chart for 15 weeks. Wong left Rock Records in July 2005 to sign with Amusic.
