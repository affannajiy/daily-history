1805 · Cape Trafalgar, Spain

Battle of Trafalgar

Horatio Nelson led a British fleet to destroy eighteen French and Spanish ships off Cape Trafalgar before dying of a gunshot wound.

Clarkson Frederick Stanfield — Public domain · source
Who
Royal Navy under Vice-Admiral Horatio Nelson
What
Defeated combined French and Spanish fleet
Where
Cape Trafalgar, Atlantic Ocean, Spain
When
21 October 1805
What happened

In October 1805, French Vice-admiral Pierre-Charles Villeneuve led a combined Franco-Spanish fleet of 33 ships of the line out of Cádiz to secure the English Channel for Napoleon Bonaparte's planned invasion of Britain. Off Cape Trafalgar, the allied fleet was intercepted by Vice-Admiral Horatio Nelson commanding a British fleet of 27 ships of the line. Outnumbered, Nelson abandoned traditional naval tactics and sailed his fleet directly at the allied battle line in two separate columns to split it into three sections.

Nelson's flagship HMS Victory led the front column under heavy crossfire, successfully breaking the Franco-Spanish line and isolating its rear from Villeneuve's flagship Bucentaure. During the fighting, Nelson was shot by a French musketeer and died shortly before the engagement concluded. The British captured or destroyed 18 allied ships without losing a single vessel, and Vice-admiral Villeneuve was taken prisoner aboard his flagship.

What changed after

The destruction of eighteen allied warships ended French plans to invade Britain across the English Channel. Vice-admiral Villeneuve was held in Britain on parole and attended Nelson's state funeral before his release, while Spanish Admiral Federico Gravina died six months later from wounds sustained in the combat.

Britain maintained naval supremacy across the seas, though the War of the Third Coalition concluded a few months later with the defeat of Britain's continental allies, allowing the Napoleonic Wars to continue for another decade.

Establishment of Azad Hind

Subhas Chandra Bose formally announced the Provisional Government of Free India in Japanese-occupied Singapore to fight British colonial rule.

Who

What
Formally established Provisional Government of Free India
Where

What happened

In March 1942, Rash Behari Bose convened a conference of Indian expatriates in Tokyo to establish the Indian Independence League and create an independence army to drive the British out of India. Subhas Chandra Bose, who was living in Germany, travelled by submarine to Japan and arrived in Tokyo on 13 June 1943 to join the movement. After arriving in Singapore on 2 July 1943, Subhas Chandra Bose assumed leadership of the Indian Independence League from Rash Behari Bose and took command of the Indian National Army.

On 21 October 1943, Subhas Chandra Bose formally declared the establishment of Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind, the Provisional Government of Free India, at the Cathay Cinema Hall in Japanese-occupied Singapore. The government asserted authority over Indian personnel in Southeast Asian British territory and created its own currency, court system, and civil code. Supported militarily and financially by Imperial Japan, Free India immediately declared war on Allied forces on the Indo-Burma Front.

What changed after

The provisional government took control of the Indian National Army and joined the Imperial Japanese Army's Fifteenth Army in the Imphal-Kohima sector, breaching British defenses at Kohima before retreating due to supply failures and Allied air dominance. Imperial Japan granted Azad Hind nominal authority over the Andaman and Nicobar Islands in 1943 until civil administration reverted to the British near the war's conclusion.

Following the defeat and surrender of INA forces in Rangoon, the British government charged 300 INA officers with treason in the INA trials. Facing intense political opposition from the Indian National Congress, the British administration backtracked on the prosecutions.

Lee Chong Wei

Born on this day, 1982

Malaysian badminton player Lee Chong Wei held the world number one ranking for 349 weeks and won three Olympic silver medals.

What they did

Lee Chong Wei was born in Bagan Serai, Perak, and began learning badminton at age 11 under local coach Teh Peng Huat before being discovered by Misbun Sidek and drafted into the Malaysian national squad in 2000. He reached his first major final at the 2003 Malaysia Open and captured his first titles in 2004 at the Malaysia Open and Chinese Taipei Open. In 2006, Lee saved eight match points against Lin Dan to win the Malaysia Open and captured two gold medals at the Commonwealth Games in both the men's singles and mixed team events.

Lee reached the world number one singles ranking for the first time in 2006 and later maintained the top spot for a record 349 weeks, including 199 consecutive weeks between 21 August 2008 and 14 June 2012. He won silver medals in men's singles at three consecutive Olympic Games in 2008, 2012, and 2016. After being diagnosed with nose cancer in July 2018, Lee announced his professional retirement on 13 June 2019 and served virtually as Malaysia's chef de mission for the 2020 Summer Olympics.

What still stands

Lee was inducted into the BWF Badminton Hall of Fame on 2 May 2023. He was awarded the title Datuk and served as chef de mission for Malaysia's delegation at the 2020 Summer Olympics.
