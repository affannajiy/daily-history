Reunification of Germany

German reunification took formal effect on 3 October 1990, establishing a single German state and creating a new national holiday.

What happened

At the beginning of July 1990, the governments of the German Democratic Republic and the Federal Republic of Germany set a schedule for East German state elections on 14 October and all-German Bundestag elections on 2 December. Facing economic and political collapse, the Volkskammer held a special session called by Minister-President Lothar de Maizière starting at 9 p.m. on 22 August 1990. Following debate, Volkskammer President Sabine Bergmann-Pohl announced at 2:30 a.m. on 23 August that 3 October would be the date of accession, aligning with the 2 October Helsinki Conference detailing Two-plus-Four talks results.

On 3 October 1990, East Germany formally ceased to exist and joined the Federal Republic of Germany, creating a single unified state for the first time since 1945. Authorities chose 3 October as the official national holiday over 9 November—the day the Berlin Wall fell in 1989—because 9 November was also the anniversary of the 1938 Nazi Reichspogromnacht pogroms.

What changed after

German Unity Day replaced the official West German holiday previously observed on 17 June from 1954 to 1990. In East Germany, Republic Day—celebrated on 7 October to mark the founding of the state in 1949—was abolished following reunification.

Alberto Ramento

Died on this day, 2006

Alberto Ramento was the ninth supreme bishop of the Philippine Independent Church who advocated for workers and human rights before his 2006 murder.

What they did

Alberto Baldovino Ramento served as the Diocesan Bishop of Cavite from 1970 to 1993 before his election as the ninth supreme bishop (Obispo Máximo) and chairperson of the Supreme Council of Bishops of the Philippine Independent Church. After his term ended, he served as the Diocesan Bishop of Tarlac. He co-chaired the Ecumenical Bishops' Forum, chaired Promotion of Church Peopleʼs Response–Central Luzon, led Karapatan–Tarlac, and chaired the Workers Assistance Center, Inc. In September 1998, the National Democratic Front of the Philippines nominated him as an Independent Observer in the Joint Monitoring Committee of CARHRIHL.

Ramento publicly criticized the Gloria Arroyo administration and advocated for the Hacienda Luisita strikers and Cavite factory workers. He also spoke out regarding the murder of priest William Tadena. After receiving death threats, Ramento was stabbed to death inside his rectory at the Church of Saint Sebastian in Tarlac City on October 3, 2006.

What still stands

The Philippine Independent Church established the Ramento Project for Rights Defenders as its human rights advocacy arm in his honor. His feast day was added to the liturgical calendar of the Union of Utrecht of the Old Catholic Churches following his 2010 canonization.
