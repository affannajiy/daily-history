1066 · Battle, East Sussex

The Battle of Hastings

William of Normandy defeated King Harold Godwinson in a decisive engagement that initiated the Norman conquest of England.

Myrabella — Public domain · source

What happened

The conflict began on 14 October 1066 when the Norman-French army led by William, Duke of Normandy, confronted the English army under Anglo-Saxon King Harold Godwinson. The battle was fought approximately 7 miles northwest of Hastings, close to modern-day Battle, East Sussex, lasting from about 9 am until dusk.

The English forces consisted almost entirely of infantry with few archers, while the invading force included cavalry, archers, and infantry. Early efforts by the invaders to break the English lines failed, prompting the Normans to use a tactic of pretending to flee in panic before turning on their pursuers. King Harold died near the end of the engagement, triggering the retreat and defeat of most of his army.

What changed after

William was crowned as king on Christmas Day 1066 after further marching and skirmishing. Rebellions and resistance to his rule continued, but the battle marked the culmination of the conquest of England. William founded a monastery at the battlefield site, where the abbey church high altar supposedly marked where Harold died.

Nobel Peace Prize Awarded to Aung San Suu Kyi

Burmese opposition leader Aung San Suu Kyi was honored with the Nobel Peace Prize while enduring political detention.

What happened

Aung San Suu Kyi, general secretary of the National League for Democracy, was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1991. She had risen to prominence during the 8888 Uprising on 8 August 1988 and subsequently led her party to win 81% of the seats in Parliament during the 1990 general election.

The military government, known as the State Peace and Development Council, refused to hand over power and nullified the election results. Having been detained before the elections, she remained under house arrest for almost 15 of the 21 years spanning from 1989 to 2010, establishing her position as a prominent political prisoner.

What changed after

The military government refused to hand over power after the 1990 election, causing an international outcry and cementing Aung San Suu Kyi's status as a political prisoner under long-term house arrest. Decades later, she became a member of the Pyithu Hluttaw in 2012 before assuming the role of State Counsellor of Myanmar in 2016.
