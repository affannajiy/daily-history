1863 · Gettysburg, Pennsylvania

Gettysburg Address

President Abraham Lincoln delivered a brief dedication address at the military cemetery in Gettysburg during the American Civil War.

Photographer attributions vary from unidentified (William Frassanito) to Mathew Brady (NARA) and David Bachrach (1845-1921) (Center for Civil War Photography). — Public domain · source

What happened

President Abraham Lincoln delivered a dedication speech on the afternoon of November 19, 1863, during the formal dedication of Soldiers' National Cemetery on the grounds where the Battle of Gettysburg had been fought four and a half months earlier. Lincoln spoke before approximately 15,000 people who had gathered to commemorate the sacrifice of Union soldiers, following a lengthy main oration by Edward Everett.

During the trip from Washington, D.C., Lincoln felt weak and dizzy, and modern clinicians believe he was likely in a prodromal period of smallpox when he spoke. His address referenced the Declaration of Independence of 1776 and lasted for less than two minutes across 271 words, focusing on the preservation of the Union and democratic governance.

What changed after

Over time, the address came to be widely viewed as one of the greatest and most influential statements delivered on American national purpose. Lincoln was subsequently diagnosed with a mild case of smallpox following his return to Washington, D.C.

What they did

Diosdado Peñalosa competed as a professional boxer from 1982 to 1995. He won the Philippine light flyweight title on November 26, 1982, through a stoppage against Romy Austria, and defended it against Alfredo Guanzon on January 30, 1983. In 1983, he became the first IBF light flyweight champion by defeating Satoshi Shingaki, defending the title three times before vacating the belt. He challenged WBA Flyweight Title holder Hilario Zapata in 1986 but lost. Peñalosa captured the IBF Flyweight Title the following year by knocking out Hi-Sup Shin, though he lost the belt in his first defense to Chang-Ho Choi and lost a split decision to Dave McAuley in 1989.

Following his retirement from competition in 1995, Peñalosa became a boxing trainer, working with Nonito Donaire. Together, the team defeated Raul Martinez on April 19, 2009, during Donaire's third title defense of his IBF Flyweight championship.

Memali Incident

Malaysian police laid siege to village houses occupied by an Islamic sect led by Ibrahim Mahmud, resulting in numerous casualties.

Who

What happened

A team of 200 policemen, acting under orders from Deputy Prime Minister and Home Affairs Minister Musa Hitam, laid siege to kampung houses in Kampung Memali, Baling, Kedah, on November 19, 1985. The houses were occupied by approximately 400 people led by Ibrahim Mahmud, also known as Ibrahim Libya, who was wanted by police under the Internal Security Act for creating discord and disharmony and harboring fugitives.

The villagers defended Ibrahim using guns, machetes, sharpened bamboo rods, and other rudimentary weapons, while police deployed heavy vehicles. The clashes resulted in the deaths of 14 villagers and 4 policemen. Police detained 159 people during the operation, including women and children.

What changed after

The Malaysian government published a white paper and broadcast an official video recording of the incident on national television station TV1. Thirty-six individuals arrested under the Internal Security Act in January 1986 were later released in June of that year. The National Fatwa Council and the Kedah State Fatwa Council issued rulings stating that those who died in the incident were not martyrs.
