1860 United States presidential election

Abraham Lincoln won the United States presidential election with less than forty percent of the popular vote after a split in the Democratic Party.

What happened

On November 6, 1860, Abraham Lincoln and his running mate Hannibal Hamlin won the United States presidential election on the Republican ticket. Lincoln, a former one-term Whig representative from Illinois, ran on a Republican platform that promised not to interfere with slavery where it already existed while opposing its expansion into federal territories. Incumbent Democratic president James Buchanan did not seek re-election, leaving four major political parties to contend for the presidency amidst regional divisions over slavery.

The election split along regional lines after the Democratic Party divided into Northern and Southern factions. Northern Democrats nominated Illinois senator Stephen A. Douglas on a popular sovereignty platform, while Southern Democrats nominated Vice President John C. Breckinridge of Kentucky. A fourth group, the Constitutional Union Party, nominated former Tennessee senator John Bell. Lincoln won 180 electoral votes and 18 states, securing a majority in the Electoral College without carrying any Southern states, while receiving 39.7 percent of the nationwide popular vote. Douglas finished second in the popular vote but carried only Missouri, Breckinridge carried 11 states, and Bell carried Kentucky, Tennessee, and Virginia.

What changed after

Lincoln's victory served as the catalyst for Southern secession. Seven Southern states that supported Breckinridge seceded from the United States prior to Lincoln's inauguration on March 4, 1861.

Less than two months after the inauguration, hostilities opened with the Battle of Fort Sumter. Following that battle, four additional slave states seceded, initiating the American Civil War and starting a run of six consecutive Republican presidential election victories.

1963 South Vietnamese coup d'état

General Dương Văn Minh led a military coup that ousted South Vietnamese President Ngô Đình Diệm following a crackdown on Buddhist protests.

What happened

On November 1, 1963, officers of the Army of the Republic of Vietnam launched a coup against President Ngô Đình Diệm and the Personalist Labor Revolutionary Party. Led by General Dương Văn Minh, the military officers acted due to disagreements with Diệm's handling of the Buddhist crisis and the military threat posed by North Vietnam and the Viet Cong. The military movement captured loyalist leaders and encountered light resistance.

The United States Kennedy administration was aware of the plot beforehand and issued Cable 243 through the Department of State to U.S. Ambassador Henry Cabot Lodge Jr., establishing a policy not to block the coup. Central Intelligence Agency liaison Lucien Conein informed the planners that the United States would not intervene and delivered 3 million Vietnamese piastres to reward participating military units. Diệm and his brother and advisor, Ngô Đình Nhu, were captured and assassinated the following day.

What changed after

The overthrow ended Ngô Đình Diệm's presidency and dismantled the ruling Personalist Labor Revolutionary Party. Military leadership under General Dương Văn Minh assumed control of South Vietnam's government following the execution of Diệm and Nhu on November 2, 1963.
