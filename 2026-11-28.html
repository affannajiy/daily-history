1943 · Tehran, Iran

Tehran Conference

Allied leaders met in Tehran to coordinate military strategy and secure a commitment for opening a second front in Western Europe.

U.S. Signal Corps photo. — Public domain · source
Who

What

Where
Soviet embassy, Tehran, Iran
When
November 28, 1943, 16:00
What happened

The Tehran Conference, codenamed Eureka, convened at 16:00 on 28 November 1943 at the Soviet embassy in Tehran, Iran. United States President Franklin D. Roosevelt, who arrived aboard the USS Iowa and traveled 11,000 kilometres, met Soviet Premier Joseph Stalin for the first time. Roosevelt was brought in a wheelchair from his adjacent accommodation, accompanied by personal emissary Harry Hopkins and Ambassador W. Averell Harriman. British Prime Minister Winston Churchill arrived half an hour later on foot alongside his general staff, including Foreign Secretary Anthony Eden and CGS Alanbrooke.

Stalin had traveled by train to Baku and subsequently flew to Tehran in an aircraft piloted by a colonel. The three leaders held negotiations through 1 December 1943, covering military strategy in Europe and Asia, operations in Yugoslavia, and relations with Turkey and Iran. Soviet Minister of Foreign Affairs Vyacheslav Molotov and military leader Kliment Voroshilov accompanied Stalin, while three Western women—Sarah Churchill, Kathleen Harriman, and Anna Boettiger—also attended the proceedings.

What changed after

The primary outcome was the commitment by the United States and the United Kingdom to open a second front against Nazi Germany, pulling German military assets away from the Eastern Front. Stalin agreed that the Soviet Union would enter the war against Japan after the defeat of Germany. The leaders also signed a separate agreement recognizing the independence of Iran.

The three leaders did not meet again in person until the Yalta Conference from 4 to 11 February 1945, followed by the Potsdam Conference held from 17 July to 2 August 1945.

Timor-Leste Declaration of Independence

Fretilin unilaterally declared independence from Portugal in 1975, triggering an Indonesian invasion and a multi-decade occupation.

What happened

Following centuries of Portuguese colonial presence administered from Dili, internal political conflict in East Timor escalated during the decolonization process in 1975. On 28 November 1975, the Revolutionary Front for an Independent East Timor (Fretilin) issued a unilateral declaration of independence, proclaiming the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste.

Nine days after the declaration, Indonesian military forces invaded the territory. Indonesia subsequently annexed East Timor as its 27th province, initiating an occupation marked by armed conflict, widespread human rights abuses, forced displacement, and severe loss of life.

What changed after

The invasion resulted in nearly 24 years of Indonesian military rule, which was not recognized by the United Nations. Resistance under Fretilin and other groups continued throughout the occupation.

On 30 August 1999, a United Nations–sponsored referendum allowed voters to reject Indonesian autonomy, leading to temporary UN administration and the restoration of full sovereignty on 20 May 2002.

Abdul Halim of Kedah

Born on this day, 1927

Sultan Abdul Halim of Kedah served twice as Yang di-Pertuan Agong, becoming the only monarch in Malaysian history to hold the office twice.

What they did

Sultan Abdul Halim Mu'adzam Shah was born on 28 November 1927 at Istana Anak Bukit near Alor Setar. He was educated at Alor Merah and Titi Gajah Malay schools and Sultan Abdul Hamid College before attending Wadham College, Oxford, where he earned a Diploma in Social Science and Public Administration. He subsequently served in the Kedah Administrative Service at the Alor Star district office and state treasury. Appointed Raja Muda on 6 August 1949, he acceded as the 28th Sultan of Kedah on 14 July 1958 upon the death of his father, Sultan Badlishah, and was installed at Balai Besar on 20 February 1959.

In July 1970, he was elected as the 5th Yang di-Pertuan Agong of Malaysia, serving from 21 September 1970 to 20 September 1975. During this term, he presided over the civilian power transfer when Prime Minister Tunku Abdul Rahman resigned in favor of Tun Abdul Razak. He served as Deputy Yang di-Pertuan Agong twice, from 1965 to 1970 and again from 2006 to 2011, before being elected to a second term as the 14th Yang di-Pertuan Agong from 13 December 2011 to 12 December 2016.

What still stands

He established Jubli Perak Park at Sungai Petani in 1983 to mark his silver jubilee as Sultan of Kedah. His reign oversaw the appointment of a Council of Regency during his second tenure as King of Malaysia to execute state duties in Kedah.
