Global Headline
1945 · Tokyo Bay, Japan

Signing of the Japanese Instrument of Surrender

Delegates from Japan and nine Allied nations gathered aboard the USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay to sign documents ending World War II.

Army Signal Corps — Public domain · source
Who
Representatives of Japan and Allied nations
What
Signed the Instrument of Surrender
Where
Aboard USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay
When
2 September 1945, 9:04 a.m.–9:25 a.m. JST
What happened

General Douglas MacArthur's staff drafted the surrender document in Manila, printing it on parchment discovered in a monastery basement. The Japanese government sent eleven delegates out to the battleship USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay, led by Foreign Minister Mamoru Shigemitsu and General Yoshijirō Umezu. The two Japanese delegates had been personally requested by Emperor Hirohito to sign the agreement.

The ceremony began shortly after 09:00 JST on 2 September 1945 and was broadcast globally. Shigemitsu signed on behalf of the Emperor and the Japanese Government at 9:04 a.m., followed by Umezu for the Japanese Imperial General Headquarters at 9:06 a.m. General MacArthur then accepted the surrender on behalf of the Allied Powers at 9:08 a.m., after which representatives from the United States, China, the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, Australia, Canada, France, the Netherlands, and New Zealand each added their signatures. The proceedings concluded at 9:25 a.m. JST.

What changed after

At 9:25 a.m. JST on 2 September 1945, World War II officially ended. Allied forces under General MacArthur assumed control of Japan's administration to execute the surrender terms across the Pacific theater.

Southeast Asia
1945 · Hanoi, French Indochina

Proclamation of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam

Following the August Revolution against the monarchy, Hồ Chí Minh declared the independence of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in 1945.

Who

What
Proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam
Where
Vietnam
When
1945
What happened

Born Nguyễn Sinh Cung in Nghệ An province, Hồ Chí Minh was educated in French schools and spent decades overseas in France, the Soviet Union, and China building revolutionary organizations. He established the Indochinese Communist Party in 1930 and returned to Vietnam in 1941 to form the Việt Minh independence movement against Japanese wartime occupation.

In 1945, Hồ led the August Revolution against the Vietnamese monarchy. Following the collapse of the imperial regime, he proclaimed the establishment and independence of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam, assuming office as the nation's prime minister.

What changed after

When French troops returned to restore colonial authority, Hồ Chí Minh's government withdrew to rural bases in 1946 to begin guerrilla warfare. The resulting First Indochina War ended with the defeat of France at Điện Biên Phủ in 1954, leading to the partition of Vietnam into North Vietnam and South Vietnam under the Geneva Conference.
