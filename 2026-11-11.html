1918 · Compiègne Forest, France

Armistice of 11 November 1918

A railroad car in northern France hosted the agreement that halted military actions across all fronts of the First World War.

What happened

Germany signed an armistice agreement with the Allies inside a railroad car located in the Compiègne Forest near the town of Compiègne. The conclusion of the agreement followed a message sent by the German government to American president Woodrow Wilson to negotiate terms based on his recent speech and the Fourteen Points. Previous armistices had already been agreed with Bulgaria, the Ottoman Empire, and Austria-Hungary as the military situation for the Central Powers deteriorated following the Battle of Amiens and the Meuse-Argonne offensive.

Officially agreed to at 5:00 a.m. by French Marshal Ferdinand Foch, the armistice came into force at 11:00 a.m. Paris Time on 11 November 1918. Fighting continued right up until the deadline, resulting in 2,738 men dying on the final day of the conflict. The terms of the agreement required the cessation of hostilities on the Western Front, the withdrawal of German forces from west of the Rhine, and the surrender of aircraft, warships, and military materiel.

What changed after

The armistice terms led to the occupation of the Rhineland by Entente forces, the release of Allied prisoners of war, and the surrender of German aircraft and warships while the naval blockade of Germany was maintained. Negotiations continued on a peace treaty, culminating in the signing of the Treaty of Versailles on 28 June 1919, which officially took effect on 10 January 1920.

Operation Commando Hunt

An aerial interdiction campaign targeted the logistical corridor running through Laos to restrict the movement of personnel and supplies.

What happened

The U.S. Seventh Air Force and U.S. Navy Task Force 77 initiated Operation Commando Hunt, a covert aerial interdiction campaign aimed at preventing the transit of People's Army of Vietnam personnel and supplies along the Ho Chi Minh Trail. The operational zone ran from southwestern North Vietnam through the southeastern portion of the Kingdom of Laos and into South Vietnam.

The campaign followed the end of Operation Rolling Thunder after President Lyndon B. Johnson decreed an end to bombing operations north of the 20th parallel on 11 November 1968. This shift redirected approximately 500 U.S. planes toward the southwest, causing bombing missions over southern Laos to climb from 4,700 sorties in October to 12,800 in November 1968.

What changed after

The redirection of aircraft led to a 300 percent increase in bombing missions over southern Laos between October and November 1968. By the end of the conflict, U.S. and South Vietnamese aircraft dropped over three million tons of ordnance on Laos.

Ismail Petra of Kelantan

Born on this day, 1949

The twenty-eighth sultan of Kelantan reigned for over three decades before his succession following a severe stroke.

What they did

Ismail Petra was born at the Istana Jahar in Kota Bharu on 11 November 1949, the son of Sultan Yahya Petra and Raja Perempuan Zainab II. He received his secondary education at Sultan Ismail College and joined the Government Administrative Services in 1968 as an officer attached to the Kelantan State Secretary Office, later working at the Land and District Office in Kota Bharu. Appointed Crown Prince on 11 November 1967, he served as Regent of Kelantan on three occasions while his father undertook federal duties. He succeeded his father as the 28th Sultan of Kelantan on 30 March 1979 and was crowned on 30 March 1980.

In addition to his administrative and royal duties, he was commissioned into the Territorial Army Regiment, eventually holding the rank of honorary lieutenant colonel, and served as the first Chancellor of Universiti Malaysia Kelantan starting on 10 August 2008. After suffering a massive stroke on 14 May 2009, a medical panel appointed by the Kelantan government deemed him incapacitated, leading to the crowning of his eldest son, Tengku Muhammad Faris Petra, as the new sultan on 13 September 2010 by order of the Kelantan Succession Council.

What still stands

Ismail Petra served as the first Chancellor of Universiti Malaysia Kelantan, an institution established in the state. His reign as the 28th Sultan of Kelantan lasted from 1979 until his incapacitation and subsequent replacement by his eldest son, Sultan Muhammad V, in 2010.
