Soviet atomic bomb project: First Soviet nuclear test

The Soviet Union detonated its first atomic bomb, yielding 22 kilotons and ending the United States' monopoly on nuclear weapons.

Who
Soviet Union
What
Detonated its first nuclear weapon, RDS-1
Where
Semipalatinsk Test Site, Kazakh SSR
When
August 29, 1949, at 7:00 a.m. Kazakhstan Time
What happened

On August 29, 1949, at 7:00 a.m. Kazakhstan Time, the Soviet Union detonated its first nuclear weapon, designated RDS-1 or First Lightning, at the Semipalatinsk Test Site in the Kazakh Soviet Socialist Republic. Designed at Laboratory № 2 starting in April 1946 under weapons developers Igor Kurchatov and Yulii Khariton, the device used plutonium manufactured at Chelyabinsk-40. Security chief Lavrentiy Beria supervised the project and insisted on using an implosion design with a solid plutonium core based on the American Fat Man bomb, detailed plans of which had been acquired through World War II espionage.

To measure the blast's effects, workers constructed wooden and brick houses, a bridge, and a simulated metro railway, while placing 100 artillery guns, 50 aircraft, and over 1,500 animals around ground zero. The explosion yielded 22 kilotons of TNT, destroying or severely damaging artillery pieces situated between 500 and 550 meters away. Test data revealed that the RDS-1 blast was 50 percent more destructive than Soviet engineers had originally calculated.

What changed after

Atmospheric radioactive debris collected on September 3, 1949, by a US Air Force WB-29 flight led US President Harry S. Truman to announce the Soviet test publicly on September 23. Thirteen days later, on October 6, 1949, Truman was briefed on developing a hydrogen bomb, prompting the United States to launch a crash program for the weapon. The Soviet Union initiated full serial production of the RDS-1 in December 1951, building a stockpile of 29 nuclear bombs.

1916 · Washington, D.C., United States

Passage of the Philippine Autonomy Act

The United States Congress passed the Jones Law, establishing the first fully elected Philippine legislature and pledging eventual independence.

Who
64th United States Congress
What
Passed the Philippine Autonomy Act (Jones Law)
Where
Washington, D.C., United States
When
August 29, 1916
What happened

On August 29, 1916, the 64th United States Congress enacted the Philippine Autonomy Act, also known as the Jones Law. Drafted in earlier forms by Manuel L. Quezon, one of two Philippine resident commissioners to the U.S. House of Representatives, and championed by House Committee on Insular Affairs Chairman William Atkinson Jones, the law replaced the Philippine Organic Act of 1902. President Woodrow Wilson signed the finalized bill after it cleared the House of Representatives, underwent amendments in the Senate, and emerged from a congressional conference committee.

The statute reorganized the Philippine Legislature into the country's first fully elected legislative body. It replaced the appointed upper house, the Philippine Commission, with an elected Philippine Senate, while renaming the elected lower house, the Philippine Assembly, as the House of Representatives. Although executive power remained under an appointed American governor general, the act contained the U.S. government's first formal declaration committing to grant independence to the Philippines once a stable government was established.

What changed after

Following the enactment of the law, elections for the newly formed Philippine Senate took place on October 3, 1916, while representatives elected on June 6, 1916, were transitioned into the new House of Representatives. The Jones Law served as the working constitution of the Philippines until the U.S. Congress enacted the Tydings–McDuffie Act in 1934.
