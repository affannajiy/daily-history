September 11 attacks

Nineteen al-Qaeda operatives hijacked four commercial airliners in 2001, executing coordinated attacks that destroyed the World Trade Center towers and struck the Pentagon.

What happened

On September 11, 2001, nineteen hijackers belonging to al-Qaeda took control of four commercial aircraft in the United States. Two of the hijacked planes were flown directly into the World Trade Center in New York City. A third hijacked aircraft crashed into The Pentagon in Arlington County, Virginia, and a fourth plane crashed into a field near Shanksville, Pennsylvania.

The coordinated strikes resulted in 2,977 deaths, damaged global financial markets, and caused long-term health consequences for nearby residents. The organization behind the hijackings, al-Qaeda, was founded in 1988 during meetings in Peshawar by Osama bin Laden, Abdullah Yusuf Azzam, Ayman Al-Zawahiri, and Mohammed Atef.

What changed after

The United States launched the war on terror following the attacks and invaded Afghanistan to depose the Taliban and dismantle al-Qaeda. In 2003, a U.S.-led coalition invaded Iraq, overthrowing the Ba'athist regime after falsely accusing it of maintaining ties to al-Qaeda.

Al-Qaeda established its regional Iraqi branch in 2004, which later morphed into the Islamic State of Iraq after 2006. U.S. forces killed Osama bin Laden in Pakistan in May 2011, after which Ayman Al-Zawahiri took control of the organization until his own death in a 2022 U.S. operation.

Born on this day, 1917

What they did

Ferdinand Emmanuel Edralin Marcos graduated cum laude from the University of the Philippines College of Law in 1939. He represented Ilocos Norte in the Philippines House of Representatives from 1949 to 1959, entered the Senate in 1959, and served as Senate president from 1963 to 1965. In 1965, running under the Nacionalista Party, he defeated Diosdado Macapagal to become the tenth president of the Philippines, securing re-election in 1969.

In 1972, Marcos declared martial law and ruled as dictator until 1981, suppressing political opponents, media outlets, and rebel groups through military force. Data from Amnesty International indicates that 70,000 people were imprisoned, 34,000 tortured, and 3,240 killed during martial law. Following the 1983 assassination of opposition leader Ninoy Aquino and widespread public discontent, Marcos called snap elections in 1986 against Corazon Aquino. Disputed results and mass protests during the People Power Revolution forced Marcos to flee to Hawaii in February 1986.

What they did

Gerald Walter Robert Templer entered the Royal Military College, Sandhurst in December 1915 and received a commission in the Royal Irish Fusiliers in August 1916. He served on the Western Front during the First World War and later fought in the Second World War. In 1952, Templer was appointed British High Commissioner in Malaya to succeed Sir Henry Gurney, who had been killed by communist guerrillas on October 6, 1951.

Templer expanded the Briggs Plan to combat the Malayan National Liberation Army, establishing internments in New Villages, declaring white and black administrative zones, and placing a $250,000 bounty on insurgent leader Chin Peng. His administration combined psychological initiatives to win hearts and minds with forced relocations, collective punishments, defoliation using Agent Orange, and the employment of Iban headhunters. He left Malaya in 1954 and served as Chief of the Imperial General Staff from 1955 to 1958, advising Prime Minister Anthony Eden during the Suez Crisis.
