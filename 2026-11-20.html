Diocletian Is Chosen as Roman Emperor

Proclaimed emperor by Roman troops in 284, Diocles took the name Diocletianus and later established the Tetrarchy to govern the empire.

When

What happened

In 284, following the deaths of Emperor Carus and his son Numerian during a campaign in Persia, Roman troops proclaimed Diocles emperor. Taking the name Diocletianus, he faced a competing claim from Carus's surviving son, Carinus, whom Diocletian defeated at the Battle of the Margus.

Diocletian restructured the administration of the empire by appointing fellow officer Maximian as co-emperor in 286 to govern the Western Empire while Diocletian ruled the Eastern Empire as senior Augustus. In 293, he expanded this administrative structure into the Tetrarchy by appointing Galerius and Constantius as junior Caesars under himself and Maximian.

What changed after

Diocletian established administrative centers at Nicomedia, Mediolanum, Sirmium, and Treverorum, shifting imperial governance closer to the frontiers. He standardized imperial taxation from at least 297, reorganized provincial divisions, enlarged the military, and initiated the Diocletianic Persecution of Christians from 303 to 312.

Garnier Expedition Captures Hanoi

French forces led by Lieutenant Francis Garnier captured Hanoi in 1873, advancing the establishment of French Indochina under the Third Republic.

What happened

In 1873, French forces commanded by Lieutenant Francis Garnier captured Hanoi from the Vietnamese. The military action formed part of the expanding French footprint in Southeast Asia during the governance of the French Third Republic.

The Third Republic had been proclaimed on 4 September 1870 following the defeat of Emperor Napoleon III in the Franco-Prussian War. Over the subsequent decades, the republican government expanded French colonial possessions in Asia and Africa, ultimately incorporating French Indochina into its colonial empire.

Francis Garnier

What changed after

The capture of Hanoi supported the formation of French Indochina, which became part of a global colonial empire that covered 13,500,000 square kilometers at its peak in the 1920s and 1930s. France maintained parliamentary governance under the Third Republic while administering colonial territories across Asia and Africa until 1940.
