Siege of Jerusalem (1187)

Balian of Ibelin negotiated the city's surrender, allowing thousands of refugees to leave Jerusalem under Saladin's terms.

Alphonse de Neuville — Public domain · source

What happened

The siege of Jerusalem began on 20 September 1187 when Saladin’s Ayyubid army arrived outside the city. Balian of Ibelin, charged with organizing the defense, faced a city filled with refugees and fewer than fourteen knights, so he created sixty new knights from squires and burgesses while storing food and money. After repelling several storm attempts, Balian negotiated with Saladin, buying safe passage for many residents. On 2 October 1187 the city was peacefully surrendered, with limited bloodshed, and Saladin restored Muslim holy sites while allowing Orthodox and Eastern Christian pilgrims free access, though Frankish pilgrims paid a fee.

What changed after

After the surrender, Saladin restored Muslim holy sites in Jerusalem and granted tolerance to Orthodox and Eastern Christian pilgrims, allowing them free access while imposing a fee on Frankish pilgrims. Control of Christian affairs was transferred to the Patriarch of Constantinople, and the Kingdom of Jerusalem’s capital moved first to Tyre and later to Acre, prompting the launch of the Third Crusade in 1189.

What happened

Presidential elections were held in South Vietnam on 2 October 1971. After opposition candidates Dương Văn Minh and Nguyễn Cao Kỳ withdrew and most opposition figures had been banned, incumbent President Nguyễn Văn Thiệu was the only candidate. Thiệu received 100% of the vote, officially marking his second term in office. These were the last presidential elections held in South Vietnam.
