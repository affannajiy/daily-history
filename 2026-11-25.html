Presentation of General Relativity Field Equations

Albert Einstein finalized a geometric theory of gravitation that explained planetary orbital anomalies and replaced Newtonian concepts of attractive force.

What happened

Albert Einstein developed general relativity between 1907 and 1915 to reconcile physical laws with electrodynamics and address limitations in existing gravitational models. In September 1905, Einstein published his theory of special relativity, introducing concepts of space and time. However, Newton's law of universal gravitation, which had described mutual gravitational attraction between masses for more than two hundred years, was inconsistent with special relativity's framework.

To resolve this inconsistency, Einstein formulated the equivalence principle using the thought experiment of an observer in a free-falling elevator experiencing weightlessness. Because an observer in free fall experiences conditions indistinguishable from an observer moving uniformly in deep space far from massive objects, Einstein established that gravity and accelerated frames of reference produce equivalent physical effects. This geometric framework culminated in 1915 with his description of how massive bodies warp spacetime.

What changed after

Einstein's theory accounted for physical phenomena unexplained by Newton's law, including minute anomalies in the orbits of Mercury and other planets. General relativity also predicted gravitational waves, gravitational lensing, and gravitational time dilation, providing the foundation for the standard Big Bang cosmological model and the understanding of black holes.

1833 · Sumatra, Dutch East Indies

1833 Sumatra Earthquake

A massive subduction earthquake along the Sunda megathrust triggered devastating tsunami waves across the Indian Ocean coastline from Sumatra to Sri Lanka.

Who
Indo-Australian and Eurasian tectonic plates
What
Magnitude 8.8–9.2 megathrust earthquake occurs
Where
Southwest coast of Sumatra, Indonesia
When
November 25, 1833, at 22:00 local time
What happened

On November 25, 1833, at approximately 22:00 local time, a massive megathrust earthquake struck off the western coast of Sumatra. The earthquake resulted from a rupture along a 1,000-kilometer segment of the Sunda megathrust trench, where the Indo-Australian plate subducts beneath the Eurasian plate. Ground shaking lasted for 5 minutes in Bengkulu and 3 minutes in Padang, indicating a large rupture source with an estimated magnitude ranging from 8.8 to 9.2 Mw.

The seismic slip displacement triggered a massive tsunami that inundated the southwestern coast of Sumatra from Pariaman to Bengkulu. The waves radiated across the Indian Ocean, causing severe destruction in the Maldives, Sri Lanka, and the Seychelles, while smaller intensity waves reached northern Australia, Thailand, and the Bay of Bengal. Casualties along the Sumatran coast were described as numerous.

What changed after

Scientific understanding of the 1833 event was reconstructed through morphological study of coral microatolls along the Mentawai islands. Researchers used Uranium-thorium dating and annual growth banding on microatolls, which cannot grow above low tide level, to measure relative sea level displacement and quantify historical uplift along the Sunda megathrust.
