1950 · Chosin Reservoir, North Korea

Battle of Chosin Reservoir

Chinese forces encircled 30,000 United Nations troops around the Chosin Reservoir in North Korea, forcing a 17-day breakout in frigid weather.

Photo by Corporal Peter McDonald, USMC — Public domain · source
Who
Chinese 9th Corps and United Nations Command
What
Chinese forces encircled and attacked UN troops
Where
Chosin Reservoir area, North Korea
When
27 November 1950 – 13 December 1950
What happened

In mid-October 1950, United Nations forces advanced into North Korea, separated by the Taebaek Mountains into the US Eighth Army on the western coast and the US X Corps with Republic of Korea forces on the eastern coast. On 19 October 1950, Chinese People's Volunteer Army formations secretly entered North Korea, with the PVA 42nd Army moving to stop eastern UN advances. Following engagements at Funchilin Pass and Wonsan, General Douglas MacArthur ordered X Corps to attack west from the Chosin Reservoir to sever the Manpojin—Kanggye—Huichon supply line, leading Major General Edward M. Almond to formulate an attack plan on 21 November 1950.

Under urgent orders from Chinese Communist Party chairman Mao Zedong to destroy UN divisions, General Song Shilun commanded 120,000 Chinese troops of the 9th Corps against the UN positions. On 27 November 1950, Chinese forces launched a surprise attack on 30,000 UN Command troops led by Major General Oliver P. Smith around Yudami-ni and Sinhung-ni. Over 17 days of fighting in freezing temperatures, encircled UN troops broke through Chinese lines and completed an evacuation by sea from the port of Hungnam.

What changed after

The withdrawal of the US Eighth Army from northwest Korea and the evacuation of the US X Corps from the port of Hungnam marked the complete withdrawal of UN troops from North Korea. Chinese forces recovered northwest Korea following the Battle of the Ch'ongch'on River and took over the territories evacuated by UN forces.

1941 · Hitokappu Bay, Kuril Islands

Departure of the Japanese Fleet from Hitokappu Bay

Japanese naval forces assembled at Hitokappu Bay and sailed toward Hawaii on 26 November 1941 to launch their attack on Pearl Harbor.

Who
Japanese Imperial Navy
What
Dispatched 24 naval vessels toward Hawaii
Where
Hitokappu Bay, Iturup, Kuril Islands
When
26 November 1941
What happened

Hitokappu Bay, a natural harbor situated on the central coast of Iturup in the Kuril Islands, served as the staging area for the Japanese Imperial Navy in late 1941. On 26 November 1941, a fleet of 24 vessels, including six aircraft carriers, departed from the bay toward Hawaii to launch an offensive against American forces.

The strike force sailed across the Pacific Ocean toward its destination. The fleet launched its attack on Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941, initiating military conflict between Japan and the United States.

What changed after

The attack launched by the fleet departing Hitokappu Bay led directly to the United States declaration of war upon Japan. Following World War II, the Soviet Union annexed the Kuril Islands from Japan, placing the harbor—renamed Kasatka Bay—under Soviet control and later Russian Federation administration.

Tanah Melayu / Malaysia
1939–2025 · Malaysia

Abdullah Ahmad Badawi

Born on this day, 1939

Abdullah Ahmad Badawi served as the fifth Prime Minister of Malaysia from 2003 to 2009 after entering parliament in 1978.

Born
26 November 1939, Bayan Lepas, Penang
Died
14 April 2025
Field
Malaysian civil service and politics
Known for
Fifth Prime Minister of Malaysia
What they did

Abdullah bin Ahmad Badawi joined the Malaysian Administrative and Diplomatic Corps in 1964 after earning a Bachelor of Arts in Islamic Studies from the University of Malaya. He served in the Public Service Department, joined the National Operations Council (MAGERAN) following the 1969 racial riots, and became Director of Youth's Ministry Department before resigning in 1978. He won election as Member of Parliament for Kepala Batas in 1978, holding the seat until 2013, and held cabinet appointments including Parliamentary Secretary and Deputy Minister of Federal Territories.

After serving as Minister of Defence, Abdullah was removed from cabinet in 1987 during the UMNO leadership dispute before returning in 1991 as Minister of Foreign Affairs. He became Deputy Prime Minister and acting UMNO deputy president in January 1999 following the removal of Anwar Ibrahim. Abdullah succeeded Mahathir Mohamad as Prime Minister of Malaysia in 2003 and served as UMNO president from 2004 to 2009. Following general election losses for the ruling coalition in 2008, he stepped down as Prime Minister in 2009 and became Chairman of the Institute of Islamic Understanding Malaysia on 1 September 2009.
