Publication of On the Origin of Species

What happened

On 24 November 1859, English naturalist Charles Darwin published On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection, or the Preservation of Favoured Races in the Struggle for Life. The work presented evidence collected during his HMS Beagle expedition in the 1830s alongside his subsequent findings from research, experimentation, and correspondence. Darwin argued that biological populations evolve over generations through a process of natural selection, presenting a body of evidence that life arose by common descent through a branching pattern.

Written for non-specialist readers, the book drew widespread interest and provoked immediate discussion among scientists, philosophers, and theologians. Before its publication, the English scientific establishment was closely connected to the Church of England, and ideas about species transmutation faced strong resistance for contradicting beliefs about a designed hierarchy and human uniqueness. T. H. Huxley and fellow members of the X Club used the surrounding debate to campaign for the secularisation of science through scientific naturalism.

What changed after

Within two decades of publication, widespread scientific consensus emerged that evolution had occurred through a branching pattern of common descent. Natural selection underwent a period of secondary credit known as the eclipse of Darwinism between the 1880s and the 1930s, during which other mechanisms gained broader support.

Between the 1930s and 1940s, the emergence of the modern evolutionary synthesis established natural selection as the central unifying concept of the life sciences.

What they did

Born in Bangkok to a family of Chinese ancestry, Samak Sundaravej studied law at Thammasat University before joining the opposition Democrat Party in 1968. He led the party's right-wing faction and became Deputy Interior Minister in 1976 after defeating M. R. Kukrit Pramoj in the general election. Later that year, he organized anti-government demonstrations preceding the 6 October 1976 massacre at Thammasat University. Following the massacre, he served as Minister of the Interior under Tanin Kraivixien, launching a campaign that caused the arrests of hundreds of alleged leftists. In 1979, he founded the right-wing Thai Citizens' Party, winning 29 of 39 seats in Bangkok in that year's general election.

Samak served as Deputy Prime Minister in 1992 and Governor of Bangkok from 2000 to 2003. In August 2007, he took leadership of the People's Power Party and became Prime Minister of Thailand in January 2008. On 9 September 2008, the Constitutional Court of Thailand disqualified him from office for hosting the commercial television cooking show Tasting, Ranting while serving as Prime Minister, ruling that receiving payments from a private company violated the constitution. After his removal, the Appeals Court upheld a two-year prison sentence against him for libel on 25 September 2008, after which he traveled to the United States for medical treatment.

What still stands

Samak founded the Thai Citizens' Party in 1979, which maintained seats in parliament through the 1980s. Following his disqualification from office by the Constitutional Court on 9 September 2008, the Thai Cabinet appointed Somchai Wongsawat as Acting Prime Minister.

Appointment of Anwar Ibrahim as Prime Minister

Opposition leader Anwar Ibrahim was appointed Prime Minister of Malaysia five days after the 2022 general election produced a hung parliament.

What happened

On 19 November 2022, Malaysia held its 15th general election following the dissolution of the 14th Parliament by Yang di-Pertuan Agong Abdullah of Pahang on 10 October 2022 at the request of Prime Minister Ismail Sabri Yaakob. The general election was the first in Malaysian federal history to result in a hung parliament. Pakatan Harapan won the largest number of seats in the Dewan Rakyat, while Perikatan Nasional won every seat in Perlis, Kelantan, and Terengganu, and all but one in Kedah. The historically dominant Barisan Nasional coalition fell to third place.

Following five days of post-election negotiations, Pakatan Harapan chairman Anwar Ibrahim secured supporting commitments from Barisan Nasional, Gabungan Parti Sarawak, the Heritage Party, the Malaysian United Democratic Alliance, Parti Bangsa Malaysia, and independent MPs. On 24 November 2022, Anwar Ibrahim was officially appointed and sworn in as the 10th Prime Minister of Malaysia by the Yang di-Pertuan Agong, while Perikatan Nasional formed the official opposition.

What changed after

The appointment established a coalition government comprising Pakatan Harapan, Barisan Nasional, Gabungan Parti Sarawak, Gabungan Rakyat Sabah, the Heritage Party, MUDA, Parti Bangsa Malaysia, and independent members of parliament. Perikatan Nasional took up the position of the official parliamentary opposition in the Dewan Rakyat.

The 2022 election marked the implementation of a constitutional amendment that lowered the voting age from 21 to 18 and introduced automatic voter registration, adding approximately six million voters to the national electoral roll.
