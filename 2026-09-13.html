1229 · Kodoe Aral, Mongol Empire

Proclamation of Ögedei Khan as Khagan of the Mongol Empire

Ögedei Khan assumed leadership of the Mongol Empire at a assembly in Kodoe Aral following a two-year regency by his brother.

Ögedei Khan

Where
Kodoe Aral, Kherlen River, Mongolia
When
13 September 1229
What happened

Following the death of Genghis Khan in 1227, his youngest son Tolui maintained the regency of the Mongol Empire for two years. Genghis Khan had previously designated Ögedei as his chosen heir prior to the 1219 invasion of the Khwarezmid Empire, after an intense conflict between his elder sons Jochi and Chagatai led to both being excluded from direct succession.

In 1229, a kurultai council convened at Kodoe Aral on the Kherlen River to formally decide the leadership of the empire. Chagatai supported his younger brother's claim in accordance with their father's wishes. After ritually declining the title three times, Ögedei was officially proclaimed Khan of the Mongols on 13 September 1229.

What changed after

After taking office, Ögedei launched military campaigns into Persia in 1230, Korea in 1231, and completed the conquest of the Jin dynasty in 1234. His commanders Batu Khan and Subutai subsequently expanded Mongol control into Eastern Europe, defeating armies at Legnica and Mohi before retreating upon Ögedei's death in 1241.

Working with civil administrators including Yelü Chucai, Ögedei instituted formal tax collection systems, developed ortogh trading networks, and established regional bureaucracies. In the 1230s, he founded Karakorum as the official capital city of the Mongol Empire.

September 1964 South Vietnamese Coup Attempt

Catholic Generals Lâm Văn Phát and Dương Văn Đức sent troops into Saigon in a coup attempt against General Nguyễn Khánh.

Who

What
Attempted coup d'état against General Nguyễn Khánh
Where
Saigon, South Vietnam
When
13 September 1964, before dawn
What happened

Before dawn on September 13, 1964, South Vietnamese Generals Lâm Văn Phát and Dương Văn Đức launched a coup attempt against the ruling military junta led by General Nguyễn Khánh. Backed by the Catholic-aligned Đại Việt Quốc dân đảng and General Trần Thiện Khiêm, rebel units entered Saigon, seized key installations, and broadcast over national radio that they had overthrown the government, with Phát promising to restore the policies of former President Ngô Đình Diệm.

General Khánh evaded capture while most senior military officers initially refrained from joining either side. Khánh gathered support with the backing of the United States, Air Marshal Nguyễn Cao Kỳ, and General Nguyễn Chánh Thi. The coup collapsed without casualties by the morning of September 14, 1964, after which Đức, Kỳ, and Thi held a press conference claiming no coup had taken place.

What changed after

Following the collapse of the coup, Khánh exiled General Trần Thiện Khiêm to Washington as ambassador and forced General Dương Văn Minh out of public life, eliminating both co-members of the ruling triumvirate. Khánh subsequently secured the acquittal of Phát and Đức at their military trial to use them as political counterweights against Kỳ and Thi.

To counter internal military opposition, Khánh sought support from Buddhist civilian activists advocating negotiations with communist forces. These political shifts alienated the United States government, leading to Khánh being ousted from power in February 1965.
