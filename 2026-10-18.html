Alaska Purchase

The United States formally assumed sovereignty over Alaska on October 18, 1867, after purchasing the territory from Russia for $7.2 million.

What happened

In 1867, U.S. Secretary of State William H. Seward entered into negotiations with Russian diplomat Eduard de Stoeckl to purchase Russian America. Emperor Alexander II had decided to sell the territory after Russia's defeat in the Crimean War, fearing the colony would be difficult to defend against British forces based in neighboring Canada during any future conflict. Furthermore, the population of sea otters had been severely depleted by fur trappers by the 1850s, decreasing the commercial value of the territory. Seward and Stoeckl agreed to a treaty for the sale on March 30, 1867.

The United States Senate ratified the bilateral treaty on April 9, 1867. American sovereignty legally took effect across the territory on October 18, 1867, with the United States acquiring 586,412 square miles (1,518,800 square kilometers) of land at an original cost of $0.02 per acre, totaling $7.2 million. Some opponents labeled the acquisition as "Seward's Folly" or "Seward's Icebox", contending that the U.S. had acquired useless land, while supporters anticipated that Alaska would serve as a base to expand American trade in Asia.

What changed after

Nearly all Russian settlers departed Alaska in the aftermath of the transaction, leaving the area sparsely populated. The acquired expanse was initially organized as the Department of Alaska in 1867, before being renamed the District of Alaska in 1884 and the Territory of Alaska in 1912.

Population growth remained low until the Klondike Gold Rush began in 1896. Ultimately, the territory became the modern-day state of Alaska in 1959.

Merpati Nusantara Airlines Flight 5601 crash

An Indonesian domestic flight crashed into Mount Puntang on October 18, 1992, killing all 31 passengers and crew.

What happened

Merpati Nusantara Airlines Flight 5601 departed Achmad Yani International Airport in Semarang at 13:05 local time on October 18, 1992, on a scheduled domestic flight to Husein Sastranegara International Airport in Bandung. Captain Fierda Basaria Panggabean served as the pilot flying alongside First Officer Adnan S. Paago as pilot monitoring. At 13:35 local time, the crew made radio contact with Bandung air traffic control at an altitude of 12,500 feet. Informed of deteriorating weather and visibility of 4 to 5 kilometers, the crew elected to proceed under visual flight rules and turned south to avoid cumulonimbus clouds.

After passing Tasikmalaya, the aircraft turned toward Garut. At 13:42 local time, Captain Panggabean requested permission to descend to 8,500 feet, which was approved in the flight's final radio contact. Approximately 16 minutes later, the CASA/IPTN CN-235-10 crashed into Mount Puntang near Mount Papandayan at 420 kilometers per hour in heavy fog and exploded, killing all 27 passengers and four crew members on board.

What changed after

Search and rescue personnel located the smoldering crash site on Mount Puntang on October 19, 1992, after walking three hours on foot. Rescuers recovered all 31 bodies and both flight recorders from the site at an altitude of 2,000 feet.

An investigation conducted by the National Transportation Safety Committee concluded that the crash was caused by pilot error aggravated by adverse weather. Flight recorder data revealed that the crew had navigated on heading 240 rather than their planned heading of 261, while winds of 25 to 40 knots increased their airspeed and brought the aircraft to the mountains earlier than expected.
