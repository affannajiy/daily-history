1989 · Berlin, East Germany

Fall of the Berlin Wall

East Germans crossed border checkpoints after a premature news announcement, prompting crowds to begin tearing down the concrete barrier separating East and West Berlin.

Who
East German citizens and border guards
What
Opening border crossings and demolishing the wall
Where
Berlin, East Germany
When
9 November 1989, late evening hours
What happened

A Politburo press conference on 9 November 1989 prematurely announced that East German citizens could travel without restrictions. News of the statement was broadcast in both East and West Berlin, prompting crowds of people to gather at city border crossings. Puzzled border guards eventually permitted the crowds to pass into West Berlin during the late evening hours as live television broadcast the events worldwide.

Over the following days, thousands of citizens travelled across the border at various checkpoints. Demolition work began soon after, with people removing obstacles and toppling the L-shaped concrete wall segments that measured four metres in height and stretched across 155 kilometres.

What changed after

Free elections held in spring 1990 demonstrated preference for German reunification and the adoption of the Western Deutsche Mark. The Deutsche Mark was introduced in July 1990, and formal unification took place on 3 October 1990 when five new states joined the ten states of the Federal Republic of Germany.

Reunited Berlin became a 16th state within Germany, while Soviet occupation forces completed their withdrawal from the country in 1994.

1293 · Trowulan, Java

Crowning of Raden Wijaya

Raden Wijaya established the Majapahit kingdom in East Java following the Mongol invasion, laying the groundwork for a major thalassocratic empire.

Who
Raden Wijaya
What
Ascending the throne as first Majapahit monarch
Where
Trik forest, Java
When
1293
What happened

Before the rise of Majapahit, the Singhasari kingdom held power on Java until Kublai Khan of the Mongol-led Yuan dynasty sent an envoy named Meng Chi to demand tribute. King Kertanagara of Singhasari rejected the demand and mutilated the envoy's ears, triggering a Mongol military expedition against Java.

Following the turmoil of the invasion, Raden Wijaya directed Madurese workers to clear the Trik forest for a new settlement. While working in the forest, laborers consumed the bitter fruit of local maja trees, providing the name Majapahit for the village and capital that became the foundation of his kingdom.

What changed after

Under queen Tribhuwana and her son Hayam Wuruk, assisted by prime minister Patih Gajah Mada, Majapahit expanded its territorial control. By 1365, the empire claimed 98 tributary territories stretching from Sumatra to New Guinea.

A civil war eventually weakened central control over vassal states, culminating in the empire's collapse in 1527 following an invasion by the Sultanate of Demak, which enabled the rise of Islamic kingdoms across Java.

1927–2014 · Lebanon and Egypt

Sabah

Born on this day, 1927

Lebanese singer and actress Sabah performed across six decades, recording thousands of songs and appearing in nearly one hundred Arab films.

Born
10 November 1927, Wadi Chahrour, Lebanon
Died
26 November 2014, Beirut, Lebanon
Field
Arabic music and cinema
Known for
Performing thousands of songs and 98 films
What they did

Jeanette Georges Feghali, known professionally as Sabah, began singing in Lebanon at age 13, releasing her first song in 1940. In the early 1940s, Egyptian actress and producer Assia Dagher invited her to Egypt. Dagher cast her in the 1945 film El-Qalb Luh Wahid (The Heart Has Its Reasons), where she adopted her character's name, Sabah, as her permanent stage name. She went on to reside in Egypt for two decades while actively starring in Egyptian cinema.

Throughout her career spanning the mid-1940s to the early 2000s, Sabah acted in 98 films, including Al lailu lana (1949), My Father Deceived Me (1951), The Second Man (1959), El Hub Keda (1961), Soft Hands (1963), Three Women (1968), and Paris and Love (1972). She recorded between 3,000 and 5,000 songs, specializing in the Lebanese folk tradition of mawwal and collaborating with Egyptian composers such as Mohammed Abdel Wahab. She became one of the first Arab singers to perform at international venues such as Carnegie Hall, the Royal Albert Hall, Olympia, and the Sydney Opera House.

What still stands

A performance at the 2011 Beiteddine Art Festival retraced her journey with over 40 singers and dancers, while the documentary series Mishwār Ḥayātī (The Journey of My Life), produced with Fadi Lubnan, recorded her career for Future Television.
