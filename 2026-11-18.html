1095 · Clermont, Auvergne

The Council of Clermont

Pope Urban II convened ecclesiastics and laymen to enact church reforms and issue a fateful military call.

Jean Colombe — Public domain · source
Who
Pope Urban II
What
Called a mixed synod and delivered a speech
Where
Clermont, Auvergne
When
17 to 27 November 1095
What happened

Pope Urban II called a mixed synod of ecclesiastics and laymen of the Catholic Church held at Clermont, Auvergne, which was at the time part of the Duchy of Aquitaine. About 300 French clerics attended the assembly, which ran from 17 to 27 November 1095. While known primarily for the speech given on the final day, the synod focused on implementing Cluniac reforms, enacting decrees, and settling local and regional issues. These measures included extending the excommunication of Philip I of France for his adulterous remarriage to Bertrade of Montfort and declaring a renewal of the Truce of God to reduce feuding among Frankish nobles.

On 27 November, Pope Urban delivered a speech that included a call to arms reacting to a request by Byzantine emperor Alexius I Comnenus for military assistance against the Seljuk Turks. Urban wrote a letter in December 1095 addressing the faithful in Flanders, lamenting that a barbaric fury had afflicted Eastern churches and grasped the Holy City of Christ in servitude. He called upon princes to free the churches of the East and appointed Adhemar of Le Puy as the leader of the expedition to set out on the day of the Assumption of Mary in 1096.

What changed after

The call to arms resulted in the First Crusade, leading eventually to the capture of Jerusalem and the establishment of the Kingdom of Jerusalem. News of the call spread by word of mouth through regions and countries of Gaul, prompting the Franks to sew crosses on their right shoulders.

Deployment of military advisors to South Vietnam

United States President John F. Kennedy escalated American involvement by sending military advisors and implementing the Strategic Hamlet Program.

What happened

John F. Kennedy served as the 35th president of the United States from 1961 until his assassination in 1963. During his presidency, he faced high tensions with communist states in the Cold War and increased the number of American military advisers, special operations forces, and helicopters in South Vietnam to inhibit the spread of communism in Southeast Asia.

In addition to sending personnel, Kennedy's administration adopted the Strategic Hamlet Program, which was implemented by the South Vietnamese government. This policy involved forced relocation, village confinement, and the separation of the rural population of South Vietnam from northern and southern communist militias.

What changed after

Vice President Lyndon B. Johnson assumed the presidency following Kennedy's assassination in Dallas on November 22, 1963. Subsequently, Congress enacted many of Kennedy's legislative proposals, including the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Revenue Act of 1964.
