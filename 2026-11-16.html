Battle of Cajamarca

Francisco Pizarro led a force of 180 men to ambush and capture the Incan emperor Atahualpa in the town plaza.

What happened

Spanish commander Francisco Pizarro arrived at Cajamarca on Friday, November 15, 1532, with 180 men and a retinue of Cañari chieftains led by Vilchumlay. Emperor Atahualpa was encamped along the heights outside the emptied town with nearly 80,000 battle-tested troops following victories against his half-brother Huáscar. Atahualpa intended to capture select Spanish specialists—including weaponsmith Juan de Salinas, horseman Hernán Sánchez Morillo, and barber Francisco López—and execute the rest of the force.

On November 16, 1532, Spanish forces executed an ambush in the central plaza of Cajamarca. The Spanish killed thousands of Atahualpa's counselors, commanders, and unarmed attendants in the plaza, seizing Atahualpa and causing his armed host outside the town to flee.

What changed after

The seizure of Atahualpa marked the opening stage of the Spanish conquest of the pre-Columbian civilization of Peru. The ambush caused Atahualpa's armed force outside Cajamarca to flee, scattering the Incan army after the loss of their imperial leadership.

Elpidio Quirino

Born on this day, 1890

Elpidio Quirino served as the sixth president of the Philippines after serving in Congress, cabinet roles, and as vice president.

What they did

Elpidio Quirino passed the 1915 Philippine Bar Examinations and was elected to the House of Representatives for Ilocos Sur's first district from 1919 to 1922. He served in the Senate from 1925 to 1935, acting as Senate Majority Leader from 1932 to 1933. In 1934, he joined the Philippine Independence Commission to Washington, D.C., securing the passage of the Tydings–McDuffie Act, and subsequently served as Secretary of Finance and Secretary of the Interior under President Manuel L. Quezon. On January 13, 1935, Quirino submitted a monetary memorandum to Governor General Frank Murphy advocating a gold-standard currency system and a central bank.

Elected Vice President alongside Manuel Roxas on April 23, 1946, Quirino served concurrently as Secretary of Foreign Affairs. Following the death of Roxas on April 15, 1948, Quirino took the presidential oath of office on April 17, 1948, at Malacañang Palace. He won a full presidential term under the Liberal Party in November 1949, confronting the Hukbalahap campaign until his defeat by Ramon Magsaysay in the November 1953 election.

What still stands

The Tydings–McDuffie Act secured by the 1934 independence commission established the legal framework for the Philippine Commonwealth and constitutional convention. Act No. 4199, enacted on March 16, 1935, established the dollar exchange standard that tied the Philippine peso to the United States dollar.
