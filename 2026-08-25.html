Global Headline
1944 · Paris, France

Liberation of Paris

French and Allied forces secured Paris after a week-long Resistance uprising and the surrender of the German garrison.

Jack Downey, U.S. Office of War Information — Public domain · source
Who
French Resistance and Free French 2nd Armoured Division
What
Liberated Paris and accepted German garrison surrender
Where
Paris, France
When
August 19, 1944 – August 25, 1944
What happened

On 19 August 1944, the French Forces of the Interior launched an uprising against the occupying German garrison in Paris, following labor stoppages across the city. Swedish Consul General Raoul Nordling brokered a brief truce between the Resistance and military governor Dietrich von Choltitz, but fighting and street barricading continued through 22 August. German engineers placed mines on Seine bridges and government buildings under directives to destroy the city, though Adolf Hitler's maximum destruction order on 23 August was not fully executed.

General Philippe Leclerc led the Free French 2nd Armoured Division into the capital after pushing forward to support the uprising. Choltitz surrendered the German garrison on 25 August 1944. General Charles de Gaulle arrived that same day at the Hôtel de Ville to establish control for the Provisional Government of the French Republic and led a victory parade down the city streets on 26 August despite snipers near the Place de la Concorde.

23 August 1944 Adolf Hitler orders the total destruction of Paris landmarks and infrastructure.
25 August 1944 Dietrich von Choltitz surrenders the German garrison to French military officers.

What changed after

The surrender restored control of the French capital to the Provisional Government of the French Republic under Charles de Gaulle, preventing the implementation of an Allied military government administration. The collapse of Vichy authorities led to a wave of extrajudicial killings of suspected collaborators in Paris before official military courts were established that autumn to conduct formal trials.

Southeast Asia
1883 · Huế, Vietnam

Treaty of Huế

France forced the Nguyễn dynasty to accept a colonial protectorate over Annam and Tonkin following a defeat at Thuận An.

Who
French Republic and Nguyễn dynasty of Vietnam
What
Signed treaty surrendering Annam and Tonkin sovereignty
Where
Huế, Vietnam
When
August 25, 1883
What happened

On 20 August 1883, French forces defeated Vietnamese defenders at the Battle of Thuận An, capturing coastal fortifications that defended the river route to Huế. Minister Nguyễn Trọng Hợp and French Bishop Gaspar subsequently arranged a 48-hour armistice requiring the Nguyễn dynasty to evacuate twelve inland forts and destroy their ammunition stores. Civil commissioner-general François-Jules Harmand then traveled to Huế aboard a steam launch to dictate terms to the imperial court.

Harmand delivered a 48-hour ultimatum threatening the destruction of the imperial capital and dynasty if the terms were rejected. Cowed by the threat of military violence, Vietnamese plenipotentiaries accepted all conditions without negotiation and signed the treaty on 25 August 1883.

Timeline
20 August 1883 French forces defeat the Vietnamese military at the Battle of Thuận An.
25 August 1883 Vietnamese plenipotentiaries sign the Treaty of Huế under a French ultimatum.
6 June 1884 France replaces the 1883 accord with the Patenôtre Accords.
Key figures
François-Jules Harmand

Nguyễn Trọng Hợp
Vietnamese Minister of Personnel

Monsignor Gaspar

What changed after

The treaty established French protectorates over Annam and Tonkin while annexing Bình Thuận province into the French colony of Cochinchina. France established the right to station a resident-general at Huế, post permanent garrisons at the Thuận An forts and Đèo Ngang mountain pass, and place the northern provinces of Nghệ An, Thanh Hóa, and Hà Tĩnh under direct French oversight.

Tanah Melayu / Malaysia
2017 · Naypyidaw, Myanmar

Terrorist Designation of ARSA

Myanmar and Malaysia formally designated the Arakan Rohingya Salvation Army as a terrorist organisation following insurgent attacks in Rakhine State.

Who
Anti-Terrorism Central Committee of Myanmar and Malaysia
What
Designated the insurgent group ARSA as terrorist
Where
Myanmar and Malaysia
When
August 25, 2017
What happened

The Arakan Rohingya Salvation Army, originally established as Harakah al-Yaqin in 2013, initiated insurgent operations in northern Rakhine State under the leadership of Ataullah abu Ammar Jununi. In October 2016, the group carried out armed attacks on military posts along the Bangladesh-Myanmar border, resulting in nine border officers and four soldiers being killed. In response, the Tatmadaw engaged in security operations that resulted in 69 insurgents killed by mid-November 2016.

Following escalated armed conflict in Rakhine State, Myanmar's Anti-Terrorism Central Committee formally declared the Arakan Rohingya Salvation Army a terrorist group on 25 August 2017 pursuant to national counter-terrorism law. Malaysia similarly classified ARSA as a terrorist entity. On 28 August 2017, the group issued a press statement denying government claims regarding foreign Islamist funding and stating its primary purpose was defending Rohingya rights.

What changed after

The designation under Myanmar's counter-terrorism legislation legally authorized state authorities to target ARSA members and assets under terror laws. Malaysia formally integrated the group into its security watchlist and counter-terrorism framework.
