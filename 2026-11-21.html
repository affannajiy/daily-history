1905 · Bern, Switzerland

Publication of Mass–Energy Equivalence Paper

Albert Einstein published a theoretical physics paper demonstrating that mass and energy are equivalent to each other.

What happened

In 1905, while employed at the Swiss Patent Office in Bern, 26-year-old Albert Einstein completed his doctoral dissertation for the University of Zurich and published four groundbreaking papers in the journal Annalen der Physik during what became known as his annus mirabilis.

Among these papers was his demonstration that mass and energy are equivalent if the special theory of relativity is correct. This work introduced the mass–energy equivalence formula, E = mc², deriving the relationship directly from his principles of special relativity.

What changed after

The 1905 paper provided theoretical foundations that Einstein later expanded in 1915 with his general theory of relativity, which incorporated gravitation into mechanics. In 1916, he published a paper applying general relativity to theoretical cosmology, introducing the cosmological constant.

On the eve of World War II, Einstein endorsed a letter to President Franklin D. Roosevelt warning of potential German nuclear research, which prompted the United States to initiate the Manhattan Project.

Establishment of the People's Action Party

Singaporean political activists founded the People's Action Party at Victoria Memorial Hall to pursue independence from colonial rule.

What happened

In 1954, political activists including Lee Kuan Yew, Toh Chin Chye, Goh Keng Swee, and S. Rajaratnam established the People's Action Party (PAP) at Victoria Memorial Hall in Singapore. Toh Chin Chye assumed office as the party's first chairman, while Lee Kuan Yew became its first secretary. The party launched as a centre-left political organization targeting postal workers, teachers, trade union members, and middle-class citizens.

Following electoral success in 1959, Prime Minister Lee Kuan Yew repositioned the party toward the political centre. In 1961, during Singapore's merger with Malaysia, Lee expelled the party's left-wing faction, which subsequently reorganized as the Barisan Sosialis.

What changed after

Following Singapore's 1965 independence, the PAP won all parliamentary seats in the 1968 general election after opposition groups boycotted the vote. The party held exclusive representation in Parliament until the Workers' Party won a 1981 by-election in the Anson Constituency.

The PAP maintained a continuous two-thirds parliamentary supermajority that allowed it to amend the Constitution. It created government-linked corporations to drive export-oriented industrialisation after the British military withdrawal in 1971.
