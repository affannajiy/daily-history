634 · Damascus, Byzantine Empire

Siege of Damascus

A bishop informed Muslim commander Khalid ibn al-Walid how to breach the walls, leading to the capture of Byzantine Damascus.

Heretiq — CC BY-SA 3.0 · source
Who
Rashidun Caliphate forces under Khalid ibn al-Walid
What
Captured Damascus from the Eastern Roman Empire
Where
Damascus, Byzantine Empire
When
19 September 634
What happened

In April 634, armies sent by Caliph Abu Bakr invaded the Byzantine Levant and defeated Roman forces at the Battle of Ajnadayn in July. Muslim commander in chief Khalid ibn al-Walid then marched north to lay siege to Damascus starting on 21 August 634. The city was protected by an 11-meter-high wall stretching 1,500 meters long and 800 meters wide, featuring six distinct gates and garrisoned under the command of Thomas, son-in-law of Emperor Heraclius.

During the siege, a bishop informed Khalid that a lightly defended section of the wall could be breached at night. Khalid mounted an assault on the Eastern Gate while Thomas negotiated a surrender at the Jabiyah Gate with Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, Khalid's second in command. Although the Muslim commanders argued over the conflicting entry methods, they ultimately honored Abu Ubayda's agreement, granting departing Roman troops three days of safe passage before initiating pursuit.

What changed after

Damascus became the first major Eastern Roman city to fall to the Rashidun Caliphate during the conquest of Syria. Three days after the surrender, Khalid ibn al-Walid pursued the departing Damascan refugees toward Antioch and engaged them six days later near present-day Al Jayyad.

2006 Thai coup d'état

Royal Thai Army tanks and special forces seized Bangkok to overthrow Prime Minister Thaksin Shinawatra while he attended the United Nations.

Who
Royal Thai Army led by Sonthi Boonyaratglin
What
Overthrew government, revoked constitution, declared martial law
Where
Bangkok, Thailand
When
19 September 2006
What happened

On the evening of 19 September 2006, the Royal Thai Army launched a bloodless coup d'état against the caretaker government of Prime Minister Thaksin Shinawatra while he was in New York City attending the UN General Assembly. Royal Thai Army Special Forces left Lopburi province at 18:30 and arrived in Bangkok by 21:00. At 21:30, military-owned television Channel 5 interrupted regular programming to play compositions by King Bhumibol Adulyadej, while army tanks took up key positions across the capital.

Organized under General Sonthi Boonyaratglin as the Council for Democratic Reform, the military junta abrogated the 1997 constitution, cancelled the national elections set for 15 October, dissolved parliament and the constitutional court, and declared nationwide martial law. Military officers arrested cabinet members present in the country, including Deputy Prime Minister Chitchai Wannasathit and Defense Minister Thammarak Isaragura na Ayuthaya.

What changed after

The junta restructured itself as the Council of National Security, enacted an interim charter, and appointed retired General Surayud Chulanont as Premier. A military-appointed tribunal subsequently dissolved Thaksin's Thai Rak Thai party and banned its executives from seeking public office for five years prior to the December 2007 elections.
