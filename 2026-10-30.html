General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade

Twenty-three nations signed a multilateral trade agreement in Geneva in 1947 to promote international trade by reducing tariffs and quotas.

What happened

During the 1940s, the United States led efforts to establish multilateral institutions for post-war trade reconstruction. Negotiations at the 1947 United Nations Conference on Trade and Employment initially aimed to establish an International Trade Organization alongside the World Bank and International Monetary Fund. However, negotiations for the organization collapsed after the United States withdrew.

Simultaneously, preparatory sessions produced a separate legal treaty focused on trade barrier reductions. On October 30, 1947, 23 nations signed the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade in Geneva, Switzerland. The agreement excluded government procurement from its scope and relied on request-and-offer procedures, most favoured nation clauses, and tariff bindings between participating countries.

What changed after

Average tariff levels for major participant nations fell from approximately 22% in 1947 to 5% after the Uruguay Round in 1999. On April 15, 1994, 123 nations met in Marrakesh to form the World Trade Organization, which officially replaced the original trade agreement on January 1, 1995.

What they did

Richard Alvarez played high school basketball for Nile C. Kinnick High School in Yokosuka, Japan, before moving to the Philippines to attend Ateneo de Manila University. As a collegiate player for the Blue Eagles, he won back-to-back UAAP MVP awards in 2000 and 2001. In 2002, he led Ateneo alongside LA Tenorio, Larry Fonacier, Wesley Gonzales, and Gec Chia to win the UAAP championship against the Green Archers. He also represented the Philippine national team at the 2003 SEA Games.

In 2004, the Shell Turbo Chargers selected Alvarez first overall in the PBA Draft. Playing 72 games in his debut season under coach Leo Austria, he received PBA Rookie of the Year honors. Throughout his playing career, he played for several PBA franchises, including the Alaska Aces, Red Bull Barako, Purefoods, Ginebra Kings, Air21, Talk 'N Text, and Kia Sorento, before transitioning into coaching as an assistant for the Macau Giant Pandas.
