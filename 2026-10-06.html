1973 · Suez Canal, Egypt

The Outbreak of the Yom Kippur War

Egyptian combat engineers breached Israeli defenses along the Suez Canal using water cannons, launching a coordinated surprise attack with Syria on October 6, 1973.

Military Battles on the Egyptian Front by Gammal Hammad. Published by Dār al-Shurūq, Egypt. — Public domain · source
Who
Egyptian and Syrian armed forces
What
Launched Operation Badr against Israeli defenses
Where
Suez Canal and Golan Heights
When
October 6, 1973
What happened

On October 6, 1973, Egyptian armed forces initiated Operation Badr by breaching the Bar-Lev Line, a network of Israeli fortifications along the eastern bank of the Suez Canal. Egyptian combat engineers deployed water cannons to blast passages through the sand wall lining the canal. Simultaneously, they operated ferries and constructed pontoon bridges to transport armored vehicles and infantry into the Israeli-controlled Sinai Peninsula, while Syrian military forces mounted a simultaneous assault against Israeli positions in the Golan Heights.

Israeli defense forces were caught unprepared by the timing and magnitude of the attack. By October 7, five Egyptian infantry divisions had established defensive bridgeheads spanning a 160-kilometer frontline along the canal. When Israeli armor reserves arrived and launched a counterattack opposite Ismailia, Egyptian troops repelled the strike using anti-tank weapons. By October 8, Egyptian forces had expanded their hold to a depth of approximately 15 kilometers along the entire eastern bank of the canal.

What changed after

By October 8, 1973, Egyptian forces secured a strip of territory 15 kilometers deep along the entire 160-kilometer eastern bank of the Suez Canal and instituted naval blockades in the Mediterranean and Red Seas. Egyptian forces repelled Israeli armor counterattacks opposite Ismailia using anti-tank weaponry.

Commemorative sites were constructed following the operation, including the 6th of October Panorama in Cairo and the October War Panorama in Damascus. Authorities in Cairo named the 6th of October Bridge after the date the military offensive launched.
