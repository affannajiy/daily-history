Global Headline
1938 · Munich, Germany

Signing of the Munich Agreement

European powers agreed to Adolf Hitler's terms to cede Czechoslovakia's fortified Sudetenland border region to Nazi Germany.

Unknown author Unknown author — CC BY-SA 3.0 de · source
Who
Nazi Germany, United Kingdom, France, and Italy
What
Signed agreement ceding Sudetenland to Germany
Where
Munich, Germany
When
30 September 1938
What happened

Germany began a low-intensity undeclared war on Czechoslovakia on 17 September 1938. In response, Britain and France formally asked Czechoslovakia on 20 September to cede the Sudetenland territory to Germany. Poland and Hungary submitted their own territorial claims on 21 and 22 September. Meanwhile, German forces conquered parts of Cheb District and Jeseník District in battles involving artillery, Czechoslovak tanks, and armored vehicles. Lightly armed German infantry briefly crossed into other border counties before being repelled. Poland assembled army units near the border and launched an unsuccessful probing attack on 23 September, while Hungary mobilized troops near the frontier without attacking. The Soviet Union announced it would aid Czechoslovakia if its Red Army could cross Polish and Romanian territory, but both nations refused.

An emergency conference convened in Munich on 29–30 September, attended by leaders of Germany, France, Britain, and Italy, while Czechoslovak and Soviet representatives were excluded from negotiations. An agreement was quickly reached on Adolf Hitler's terms. The mountainous Sudetenland borderland had served as a natural border between the Czech state and Germanic states since the early Middle Ages and was fortified to block German invasion. On 30 September, Czechoslovak President Edvard Beneš submitted to diplomatic pressure from Britain and France along with military pressure from Germany, Poland, and Hungary, agreeing to surrender the Sudetenland following the terms of the agreement.

What changed after

On 2 November 1938, the First Vienna Award separated Hungarian-inhabited territories in southern Slovakia and southern Subcarpathian Rus' from Czechoslovakia. On 30 November 1938, Czechoslovakia ceded small patches of land in the Spiš and Orava regions to Poland. In March 1939, the First Slovak Republic proclaimed independence as a German puppet state, and Hitler occupied the remainder of the country, establishing the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia. The annexation provided Nazi Germany with Czechoslovakia's armaments industry, tanks, and military equipment.

2009 Sumatra Earthquake

A powerful magnitude 7.6 undersea earthquake struck off West Sumatra, destroying tens of thousands of homes and killing over 1,000 people.

Who
Indonesian authorities, rescue teams, and international agencies
What
Responded to a 7.6 Mw earthquake
Where
Off the coast of Sumatra, Indonesia
When
30 September 2009, 17:16:10 local time
What happened

A magnitude 7.6 earthquake struck off the coast of West Sumatra at 17:16:10 local time on 30 September 2009. The epicenter was located 45 kilometers west-northwest of Padang and 220 kilometers southwest of Pekanbaru at a depth of 71 kilometers. Tremors were felt as far away as Jakarta, Malaysia, and Singapore, where high-rise office buildings were evacuated. Government authorities confirmed 1,115 deaths, with Padang Pariaman recording 675 fatalities, Padang recording 313, Agam recording 80, and Pariaman recording 37. Severe injuries reached 1,214, with another 1,688 people suffering light injuries.

The quake destroyed or damaged thousands of structures across West Sumatra, including at least two hospitals, several schools, and major hotels in Padang. Landslides occurred in the hills surrounding Lake Maninjau, while a landslide at Gunung Nan Tigo in Padang Pariaman district wiped out entire villages. Minangkabau International Airport suffered ceiling damage in its boarding area. Emergency teams from the National Search and Rescue Agency and the Indonesian military deployed earth-moving equipment, while local villagers searched collapsed concrete buildings by hand to rescue trapped survivors.

What changed after

Around 250,000 families, totaling 1,250,000 people, were affected through the loss of homes or livelihoods, with 135,000 houses severely damaged, 65,000 moderately damaged, and 79,000 slightly damaged. International relief organizations including World Vision, Oxfam, IFRC, Muslim Charity, and Mercy Corps dispatched response teams to West Sumatra, with World Vision issuing a $1 million appeal and distributing 2,000 collapsible water containers. On 5 October 2009, Indonesian authorities shifted operations from survivor rescue to clearing rubble and airdropping food and blankets by helicopter to remote communities.
