What happened

Alexander III of Macedon ascended the throne at age 20 after his father Philip II was assassinated in 336 BC. Tutored by Aristotle until age 16, he secured control of Macedon by launching a campaign in the Balkans in 335 BC, reasserting authority over Thrace and Illyria before destroying Thebes. In 334 BC, he initiated a pan-Hellenic invasion of the Achaemenid Persian Empire.

His forces conducted campaigns throughout Asia Minor and Egypt, overthrowing Darius III following victories at Issus and Gaugamela. During his presence in Egypt, Alexander founded more than twenty cities bearing his name, most notably Alexandria. He pushed his campaigns as far east as Punjab, defeating King Porus at the Hydaspes in 326 BC before his troops forced a return at the Beas River.

What changed after

Following Alexander's death in Babylon in 323 BC, civil wars broke out across the empire, leading to its disintegration under the Diadochi. Greek colonies established during his campaigns introduced Hellenistic culture and the Greek language across the region stretching to the Indian subcontinent.

Greco-Buddhism and Hellenistic Judaism emerged through cultural syncretism in the conquered territories. The Greek language subsequently served as the regional lingua franca and remained the predominant language of the Byzantine Empire until 1453 AD.

Battle of Ia Drang

American and North Vietnamese forces engaged in their first major conflict in the central highlands at the foot of Chu Pong Massif.

Who

What

Where

What happened

On November 14, 1965, the 1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry Regiment under Lieutenant Colonel Hal Moore landed at LZ X-Ray near the eastern foot of the Chu Pong Massif. Surrounded by numerically superior People's Army of Vietnam forces, the American troops held LZ X-Ray through November 16 using artillery bombardments and tactical support from Boeing B-52 Stratofortress strategic bombers. American commanders claimed LZ X-Ray as a tactical victory, citing a 10:1 kill ratio.

On November 17, the engagement shifted to LZ Albany farther north, where Lieutenant Colonel Robert McDade led the 2nd Battalion, 7th Cavalry Regiment. North Vietnamese troops ambushed the battalion at close quarters, preventing the deployment of close air and artillery support. American forces suffered a casualty rate exceeding 50% before extricating themselves on November 18, leading both sides to claim victory.

What changed after

The battle established operational patterns for the conflict, with American commanders relying on air mobility, artillery, and close air support while North Vietnamese troops adopted close-range tactics to neutralize air power.

The combat at LZ X-Ray was documented in the CBS report Battle of Ia Drang Valley by Morley Safer and the book We Were Soldiers Once... And Young. In 1994, former combatants returned to the site for the ABC News documentary They Were Young and Brave.

Born on this day, 1979

What they did

Miguel Sabah Rodríguez began his professional career with Club Deportivo Guadalajara, making his debut on 12 August 2000 against Cruz Azul. He scored 18 goals in 86 appearances over nine seasons with Guadalajara before transferring to Cruz Azul in 2006. At Cruz Azul, he scored 11 goals in the Apertura 2007 season and helped the team reach two consecutive league finals in 2008.

On 28 December 2008, Sabah transferred to Monarcas Morelia, where he scored 64 goals in three years. He later returned to Chivas in 2013 and joined Club León in 2014, winning the Liga MX championship with León. On the international stage, Sabah earned 17 caps for the Mexico national team, finishing as top scorer of the 2009 CONCACAF Gold Cup and scoring the 500th goal in Gold Cup history against Panama.
