Cuban Missile Crisis

American reconnaissance flights revealed secret Soviet nuclear installations in Cuba, triggering a tense naval blockade.

What happened

In October, a U-2 spy plane captured photographic evidence of medium- and long-range nuclear launch facilities under construction in Cuba. President John F. Kennedy convened the Executive Committee of the National Security Council (EXCOMM) to weigh potential military responses against the Soviet deployment.

While advisers advocated for an air strike and subsequent mainland invasion, Kennedy chose a naval blockade to avoid a declaration of war. On 22 October, he announced the operation, specifically terming it a quarantine to bypass the formal legal implications of a state of war.

What changed after

The Soviet Union agreed to dismantle its offensive weapons in Cuba under United Nations verification, while the United States made a public declaration not to invade Cuba. Additionally, the United States secretly agreed to dismantle all offensive weapons deployed to Turkey, and all Thor missiles in the UK were disbanded by August 1963.

The necessity for direct communication led to the establishment of the Moscow-Washington hotline. Subsequent agreements reduced US-Soviet tensions for several years.

Bảo Đại

Born on this day, 1913

Nguyen dynasty monarch Bảo Đại ruled as the final emperor of Vietnam through shifting French and Japanese administrations.

What they did

Born Nguyễn Phúc Vĩnh Thụy on 22 October 1913, Bảo Đại succeeded to the throne in 1925 following the death of Emperor Khải Định, performing his enthronement ceremony on 8 January 1926. He spent much of his youth studying in France at institutions such as the Lycée Condorcet, Hattemer School, and Sciences Po before returning to assume active rule in 1932.

After Japanese forces ousted the Vichy French administration in March 1945, he retained the throne under the proclaimed Empire of Vietnam until his abdication in August 1945 following the Japanese surrender. Years later, he served as chief of state of the State of Vietnam from 1949 to 1955, before being ousted in a 1955 referendum by Prime Minister Ngô Đình Diệm.
