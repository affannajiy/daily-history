1066 · Stamford Bridge, England

Battle of Stamford Bridge

English forces launched a surprise attack against Norwegian invaders, killing King Harald Hardrada and ending his attempt to seize the English crown.

What happened

In September 1066, King Harald Hardrada of Norway invaded northern England after Tostig Godwinson, the exiled earl of Northumbria and brother of English king Harold Godwinson, pledged allegiance to him. Sailing with 300 longships and carrying 10,000 troops, Harald aimed to claim the English throne. On September 20, his invading force defeated the local regional armies of Northumbria and Mercia at the Battle of Fulford near York.

Five days later, on September 25, 1066, King Harold Godwinson launched a surprise attack against the Norwegian forces at the Battle of Stamford Bridge. Harald Hardrada was killed during the battle, and his army was wiped out, bringing a sudden end to the invasion.

What changed after

The defeat at Stamford Bridge wiped out Harald Hardrada's army and permanently ended Norwegian efforts to claim the English crown. Historians consider Harald's death during this battle to mark the end of the Viking Age.

Born on this day, 1879

Died

Field

What they did

Lope K. Santos was born in Pasig on September 25, 1879. He studied at the Escuela Normal Superior de Maestros and Colegio Filipino before joining the revolutionaries during the Philippine Revolution of 1896. He later pursued law at the Academia de la Jurisprudencia and the Escuela de Derecho de Manila, graduating with a Bachelor of Arts degree in 1912. In 1900, he launched his newspaper Ang Kaliwanagan, and in 1903 he absorbed the Unión Obrera Democrática Filipina into his union, the Unión del Trabajo de Filipinas. Between 1903 and 1906, he serialized and published his novel Banaag at Sikat, the first socialist-oriented book in the Philippines.

Santos entered public service as governor of Rizal in 1910 and governor of Nueva Vizcaya from 1918 to 1920. He then served in the 5th Philippine Legislature as a senator representing non-Christian majorities, authoring Philippine Legislature Act No. 2946 to declare November 30 as Bonifacio Day. In 1940, he published Balarila ng Wikang Pambansa, the first grammar book for the national language, before serving as Director of the Surian ng Wikang Pambansa from 1941 to 1946. In 1945, he translated the 1935 Constitution for UNESCO.

What still stands

Santos authored Philippine Legislature Act No. 2946, which designated November 30 as Bonifacio Day, an official annual public holiday. His 1940 publication Balarila ng Wikang Pambansa established the standard grammar textbook commissioned by the Surian ng Wikang Pambansa.
