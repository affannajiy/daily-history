1939 · Moscow, Soviet Union

Signing of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact

Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union agreed to a ten-year non-aggression pact containing a secret protocol partitioning Eastern Europe between them.

Template:Helmut Laux — CC BY-SA 3.0 de · source
Who
Vyacheslav Molotov and Joachim von Ribbentrop
What
Signed non-aggression pact and secret partition protocol
Where
Moscow, Soviet Union
When
August 23, 1939
What happened

In Moscow, Soviet Premier and Foreign Minister Vyacheslav Molotov and German Foreign Minister Joachim von Ribbentrop signed the Treaty of Non-Aggression between Germany and the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics on 24 August 1939, backdated to 23 August. Rapprochement between the two powers began earlier in 1939 after tripartite talks between the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom, and France broke down following Soviet exclusion from the September 1938 Munich Agreement. Under the terms of the agreement, both nations committed to refrain from aiding or allying with an enemy of the other for 10 years.

Attached to the treaty was a Secret Additional Protocol that established German and Soviet spheres of influence across Eastern Europe. The secret protocol agreed to partition Poland between the two regimes. Latvia, Estonia, Finland, and Bessarabia were allotted to the Soviet sphere, while Lithuania was designated to the German sphere before being reassigned to the Soviet sphere in a subsequent boundary treaty signed on 28 September 1939.

What changed after

Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939, followed by the Soviet invasion of Poland on 17 September 1939. In March 1940, the Soviet Union annexed parts of Karelia, Salla, and Kuusamo following the Winter War against Finland, and subsequently annexed Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Bessarabia, Northern Bukovina, and the Hertsa region.

The Polish territories annexed by the Soviet Union east of the Curzon line remained part of the USSR after World War II and were incorporated into modern Belarus and Ukraine. The core of Bessarabia became Moldova, while parts of Karelia, Salla, Kuusamo, Estonian Ingria, Petseri County, and Abrene remained in Russia after the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991. Ribbentrop was convicted of war crimes at the Nuremberg trials and executed in 1946, while Molotov was removed from power during de-Stalinization and died in 1986.

Southeast Asia
2010 · Manila, Philippines

Manila Hostage Crisis

A dismissed Philippine police officer hijacked a tour bus carrying 25 people in Manila, resulting in a standoff that left nine dead.

Who
Dismissed senior inspector Rolando Mendoza
What
Hijacked tour bus and held hostages
Where
Rizal Park, Manila, Philippines
When
August 23, 2010
What happened

On the morning of August 23, 2010, 55-year-old former Philippine National Police senior inspector Rolando Mendoza hijacked a Hong Thai Travel Services tour bus carrying 25 people, including 21 tourists from Hong Kong and four Filipinos, at Rizal Park in Manila. Armed with an M16 rifle and a handgun, Mendoza demanded reinstatement to his police post with benefits following his dismissal in February 2009. Tour guide Masa Tse Ting-chunn phoned his agency in Hong Kong shortly after 10:30 am to report the hijacking, which was subsequently broadcast live by international television news networks.

Negotiations led by police officials resulted in the release of nine hostages in stages, leaving 17 people aboard the vehicle. The standoff broke down about ten hours into the crisis after the Office of the Ombudsman rejected Mendoza's demand for immediate reinstatement and police arrested his brother, SPO2 Gregorio Mendoza, near the scene for carrying an unauthorized gun. Watching the live broadcast from a television inside the bus, Mendoza fired upon the remaining hostages after seeing his brother arrested. Police officers stormed the vehicle through a broken back window and killed Mendoza in a shootout.

What changed after

Investigations conducted by both the Philippine and Hong Kong governments concluded that poor handling by Philippine officials contributed to the deaths of the eight hostages. The tactical response mounted by the Manila Police District was widely criticized, prompting the Hong Kong Government to issue a black travel alert for the Philippines.

President Benigno Aquino III declined to issue an official apology, stating that the action of one individual should not represent the whole country. On April 12, 2018, President Rodrigo Duterte publicly apologized for the incident on behalf of the Philippines during a state event.
