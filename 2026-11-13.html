1970 Bhola cyclone

A catastrophic tropical cyclone struck East Pakistan, killing up to 500,000 people and fueling political disillusionment across the Ganges Delta.

NOAA — Public domain · source
Who
Pakistan Meteorological Department and central government
What
Tropical cyclone struck low-lying Ganges Delta
Where
East Pakistan and West Bengal, India
When
November 12, 1970, evening
What happened

The storm originated on November 8, 1970, in the central Bay of Bengal from the remnants of Tropical Storm Nora. Moving northward, the system intensified into a severe cyclonic storm on November 11, reaching peak intensity with three-minute sustained winds of 185 km/h (115 mph) and one-minute sustained winds of 240 km/h (150 mph). On the evening of November 12, the cyclone made landfall along the coastline of East Pakistan during high tide, pushing a storm surge over the low-lying islands of the Ganges Delta. The system degraded to a cyclonic storm near Agartala on November 13 and dissipated into a remnant low over southern Assam that evening.

The storm caused between 300,000 and 500,000 deaths, primarily from drowning due to the surge. In the upazila of Tazumuddin, over 45% of the 167,000 residents died. The Pakistani central government, led by junta leader General Yahya Khan, faced severe public criticism from East Pakistani leaders and international media over its delayed relief operations.

What changed after

Disillusionment with the West Pakistan government's disaster response helped the opposition Awami League secure a landslide election victory in East Pakistan one month after the storm. Seven months after the election, the political crisis escalated into the Bangladesh Liberation War.

Impeachment of President Joseph Estrada

House Speaker Manny Villar transmitted articles of impeachment against President Joseph Estrada, initiating a constitutional trial in the Philippines.

What happened

Manuel Villar Jr. served as Speaker of the House of Representatives of the Philippines from 1998 to 2000. Representing the district of Las Piñas, he directed legislative business through the lower chamber during a period of escalating political opposition against the executive branch.

During his tenure as Speaker, Villar presided over the House of Representatives as it formally considered and passed articles of impeachment against sitting President Joseph Estrada. The transmission of these articles transferred the case to the Senate of the Philippines for trial.

What changed after

Villar ended his term in the House of Representatives in 2001 and won a seat in the Senate of the Philippines, serving until 2013. He later held the office of Senate President from 2006 to 2008 and ran for President of the Philippines in 2010 under the Nacionalista Party, losing to Benigno Aquino III.

What they did

Kandiah Kamalesvaran was born on 13 November 1934 in Brickfields, Kuala Lumpur, to Ceylonese Tamil parents. He lived in Jaffna, Ceylon, from 1936 to 1938 before returning to Malaya, where he attended the Victoria Institution and lived through the Japanese occupation. In April 1953, he moved to South Australia to finish high school at King's College and enroll in architecture at the University of Adelaide. While studying, he entered Australia's Amateur Hour on television and won the state final, leading him to abandon architecture for a career in music.

Media executive Rupert Murdoch sponsored Kamahl in December 1958 by booking him for a six-week season at the Australia Hotel in Sydney. Kamahl released his first album, A Voice to Remember, in October 1967. Over his career, he released more than 30 albums and performed at venues including the Sydney Opera House, the London Palladium, and Carnegie Hall. His 1975 single "The Elephant Song" reached number one on the charts in the Netherlands and Belgium.
