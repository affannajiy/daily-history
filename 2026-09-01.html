Invasion of Poland

What happened

On the morning of September 1, 1939, German and Slovak troops launched an invasion of Poland from the north, south, and west following the Gleiwitz incident. The attack followed the Danzig crisis and the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, which Germany and the Soviet Union had signed one week earlier. As the Wehrmacht advanced, Polish forces withdrew from their border positions to defensive lines farther east. On September 3, the United Kingdom and France declared war on Germany under their alliance agreements with Poland, though France's Saar Offensive provided very limited support.

Following the Polish defeat in the Battle of the Bzura in mid-September, Polish troops prepared for a defense of the Romanian Bridgehead in the southeast. On September 17, the Soviet Red Army invaded eastern Poland beyond the Curzon Line under the secret protocol of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact. Facing two fronts, the Polish government ordered an emergency evacuation of all troops into neutral Romania. The campaign ended on October 6 following the Polish defeat at the Battle of Kock.

What changed after

On October 6, 1939, German and Soviet forces established complete control over Poland, ending the Second Polish Republic. On October 8, Germany directly annexed western Poland and the former Free City of Danzig, transferring remaining Polish territory to the General Government. The Soviet Union incorporated its annexed eastern regions into the Byelorussian and Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republics.

In response to the conquest, resistance groups established the Polish Underground State within the former national territory. Escaped Polish military personnel evacuated through neutral Romania and joined the Polish Armed Forces in the West under the Polish government-in-exile.
