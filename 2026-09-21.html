French Revolution: The National Convention abolishes the monarchy

France became a republic as its newly elected single-chamber assembly convened in Paris to dismantle the centuries-old monarchy.

Who
National Convention
What
Abolished the monarchy and established a republic
Where
Salle du Manège, Paris, France
When
September 21, 1792
What happened

The National Convention was established after the insurrection of 10 August 1792, following a decree by the Legislative Assembly that provisionally suspended King Louis XVI. To draft a new constitution without a monarch, elections were arranged under universal male suffrage for Frenchmen aged 21 or older who had resided in France for a year and lived by their labor. Primary assemblies gathered on 26 August to select electoral colleges, which held indirect elections between 2 and 10 September.

In Paris, Maximilien Robespierre presided over the election process, collaborating with the radical press to exclude royalist candidates. Across France, turnout reached 11.9% of the voting population, and only eleven primary assemblies sought to maintain the monarchy. The 749-deputy assembly held its first meeting on 20 September 1792 in the Salle des Cent-Suisses. The following day, September 21, the assembly relocated to the Salle du Manège and formally abolished the French monarchy, organizing the first French government structured as a republic.

What changed after

Power within the assembly later shifted from the full body to the Committee of Public Safety in April 1793. From the fall of 1793 to the spring of 1794, Maximilien Robespierre and his faction governed through the Committee of Public Safety during the Reign of Terror.

Following the fall of Robespierre, the assembly maintained governance until 26 October 1795 (4 Brumaire IV), when a newly drafted constitution replaced the National Convention with the French Directory.

Philippine president Ferdinand Marcos begins authoritarian rule by declaring martial law

Philippine president Ferdinand Marcos suspended constitutional democracy and declared martial law to establish a decade of authoritarian rule.

What happened

Ferdinand Marcos won the Philippine presidency in 1965 and secured re-election in 1969. His second administration faced rising public debt, inflation, and public demonstrations connected to Philippine participation in the Vietnam War. In 1972, Marcos declared martial law across the archipelago, suspending democratic procedures and consolidating government power under his presidential office through a framework he described as constitutional authoritarianism.

Under martial law, Marcos altered the constitution to expand executive authority, shut down independent news organizations, and arrested opposition politicians. The regime deployed military units against communist groups, Muslim factions, and political critics under the framework of his Movement for a New Society (Bagong Lipunan). Amnesty International data recorded that state forces imprisoned 70,000 individuals, tortured 34,000, and killed 3,240 people during the martial law era.

What changed after

A military-supervised plebiscite ratified the martial law structure and revised constitution in 1973. Marcos maintained dictatorial authority until formally lifting martial law in 1981, after which he won a third presidential term.

Following the 1983 assassination of opposition leader Ninoy Aquino and a severe economic downturn, political opposition grew until snap elections in February 1986 triggered the People Power Revolution. The uprising forced Marcos to flee to Hawaii, elevating Corazon Aquino to the presidency.
