Discovery of Tutankhamun's Tomb

British archaeologist Howard Carter uncovered stone steps leading to the mud-plastered entrance of Pharaoh Tutankhamun's intact tomb in Egypt.

What happened

In 1914, Lord Carnarvon received the concession to excavate in the Valley of the Kings and selected Howard Carter to lead a systematic search for unexcavated royal tombs, specifically targeting the tomb of Pharaoh Tutankhamun. The First World War interrupted their effort while Carter served as a diplomatic courier and translator for the British Government. Carter resumed digging in late 1917, but after several unsuccessful seasons, Carnarvon prepared to stop funding. Following a discussion, Carnarvon agreed to finance one final season of work.

Carter returned to the Valley of the Kings and directed his crew to clear rock debris beneath a line of abandoned workmen's huts. On 4 November 1922, a worker clearing ground beneath the huts uncovered a rock-cut step. Carter supervised further digging until a staircase exposed the top of a mud-plastered doorway stamped with indistinct hieroglyphic cartouches. Carter ordered the staircase refilled for protection and telegraphed Carnarvon, who arrived from England on 23 November 1922 alongside his daughter Lady Evelyn Herbert.

What changed after

Following the unearthing of the doorway, Lord Carnarvon and Lady Evelyn Herbert reached the site on 23 November 1922 to witness the clearance of the entrance staircase. The site was recorded as the best-preserved pharaonic tomb discovered in the Valley of the Kings.

Handover of Binh Thuy Air Base

The United States Air Force completed the transfer of Binh Thuy Air Base in the Mekong Delta to South Vietnamese military control.

What happened

Construction of Binh Thuy Air Base began in February 1964 on low-lying swampland adjacent to the Bassac River near Cần Thơ to replace an airfield at Sóc Trăng. United States Navy Seabees and construction contractor RMK-BRJ moved over 680,000 cubic yards of fill to build a 6,000-foot asphalt runway, taxi ramps, and headquarters buildings. USAF units began operating from the base in May 1965, stationing observation aircraft, rescue helicopters, and gunships to cover operations in the Mekong Delta.

In February 1970, under the policy of Vietnamization, the United States Air Force initiated the transfer of Binh Thuy Air Base to the Republic of Vietnam Air Force 4th Air Division. USAF units withdrew or relocated, including the 22nd Tactical Air Support Squadron, which moved to Bien Hoa Air Base in January 1970. The complete handover of the airfield to South Vietnamese control was finished by the end of 1970.

What changed after

Operational responsibility for Binh Thuy Air Base passed entirely to the RVNAF 4th Air Division by the end of 1970. On 11 April 1972, the adjacent US Navy installation was transferred to the Republic of Vietnam Navy, and the air base remained operational until South Vietnamese jets launched the final strike of the war on 30 April 1975, destroying two T-54 tanks of the People's Army of Vietnam 10th Division.

Ismail Abdul Rahman

Born on this day, 1915

Ismail Abdul Rahman served as Deputy Prime Minister of Malaysia and became the first Malay to earn a medical degree from Melbourne.

What they did

Ismail bin Abdul Rahman was born on 4 November 1915 in Johor Bahru. His father, Abdul Rahman Mohamed Yassin, served as the first President of the Dewan Negara and chairman of Malayan Banking. Ismail attended English College Johore Bahru, studied at King Edward VII College of Medicine in Singapore from 1936 to 1939, and graduated from the University of Melbourne in 1945 with a Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery degree. Upon returning to Malaya, he established the Tawakkal Clinic in Johor Bahru, operating it from 1947 to 1953, and co-founded the Malay Graduates' Association.

After being appointed to the Johor State Legislative Assembly by Chief Minister Onn Jaafar, Ismail initially opposed the UMNO-supported Federation of Malaya framework before joining UMNO when the party committed to independence. He held cabinet portfolios, serving as Minister of Home Affairs during the 13 May 1969 racial riots. In September 1970, he assumed office as the second Deputy Prime Minister of Malaysia under Prime Minister Tun Abdul Razak, holding the post until his death on 2 August 1973.
