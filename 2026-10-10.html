Battle of Tours

What happened

In 732, Charles Martel led Frankish troops against an Umayyad military invasion advancing into Aquitaine. At the Battle of Tours, his forces successfully engaged and defeated the invading Umayyad army, halting the spread of Islam in Western Europe.

Before this engagement, Charles had consolidated power across Francia following the death of his father, Pepin of Herstal, in December 714. After escaping imprisonment in Cologne by his stepmother Plectrude in 715, Charles was acclaimed mayor of the palace over Austrasia and built a reputation as an effective military commander in battles such as Amblève in April 716.

What changed after

Following his victory at Tours, Charles Martel maintained his position as de facto ruler of the Franks, having restored centralized government across Francia and re-established Frankish dominion over Gaul. At the end of his reign, he divided Francia between his sons, Carloman and Pepin.

Pepin became the first king of the Carolingian dynasty. Pepin's son Charlemagne expanded the Frankish realms further and became the first emperor in Western Europe since the Fall of the Western Roman Empire.

What they did

Born Ye Htut on 16 January 1516 to Mingyi Swe and Shin Myo Myat, he was educated in the Toungoo Palace alongside Prince Tabinshwehti after his mother was selected as wet nurse for the royal heir. Receiving training in military strategy, martial arts, horseback riding, and elephant riding, Ye Htut became the prince's chief deputy. Following Tabinshwehti's reign, Bayinnaung ascended the throne on 30 April 1550 as King of Burma within the Toungoo dynasty.

During his 31-year reign, Bayinnaung conquered the Shan States between 1557 and 1563 and expanded his authority across Lan Na, Lan Xang, Manipur, and the Ayutthaya Kingdom. He reformed the administration of the Shan States by diminishing the hereditary authority of local saophas and aligning regional customs with lowland Burmese governance. Operating under the Mandala model, he held his empire together through personal allegiances as a Chakravartin rather than central bureaucratic institutions.

What still stands

The administrative system integrating the Shan States into the Irrawaddy valley governance outlived Bayinnaung and was continued by Burmese monarchs until 1885. Modern landmarks in Myanmar carry his name, and Thai historical tradition designates him as the Conqueror of the Ten Directions.

Launch of Soyuz TMA-11

Medical officer Sheikh Muszaphar Shukor launched aboard Soyuz TMA-11 to become the first Malaysian to enter space and visit the International Space Station.

Sheikh Muszaphar Shukor and Expedition 16
What
Launched aboard Soyuz TMA-11 to ISS
Where
International Space Station
When
10 October 2007 at 13:22 UTC
What happened

On 10 October 2007, at 13:22 UTC (9:21 pm Malaysia time), orthopaedic medical officer Sheikh Muszaphar Shukor launched aboard the Soyuz TMA-11 spacecraft to the International Space Station alongside Expedition 16 crew members Yuri Malenchenko and Peggy Whitson. His spaceflight was arranged through the Angkasawan program under an agreement between Malaysia and Russia linked to Malaysia's purchase of 18 Russian Sukhoi Su-30 MKM fighter jets. Chosen as the prime crew member after 18 months of training at Star City in Russia, Sheikh Muszaphar carried out scientific experiments in orbit on liver cancer cells, leukaemia cells, and protein crystallization.

Because his 11-day mission coincided with Ramadan, the Islamic National Fatwa Council issued an 18-page manual titled Guidelines for Performing Islamic Rites (Ibadah) at the International Space Station to advise on low-gravity prayer routines and Mecca orientation. On 13 October 2007, Sheikh Muszaphar celebrated Eid ul-Fitr aboard the station by sharing satay and cookies with the crew. He returned to Earth on 21 October 2007 aboard Soyuz TMA-10 alongside Expedition 15 members Fyodor Yurchikhin and Oleg Kotov, landing west of Arkalyk, Kazakhstan, at 10:36 GMT following a ballistic re-entry caused by a damaged control panel cable.

What changed after

The mission led the Islamic National Fatwa Council to publish the 18-page manual Guidelines for Performing Islamic Rites (Ibadah) at the International Space Station, establishing protocols for prayer times, Mecca orientation, and fasting in low-gravity environments.

For the 2008 ASEAN University Games, the organizing committee selected an astronaut mascot named A1 Angkasawan in recognition of the spaceflight.
