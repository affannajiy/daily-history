Formation of OPEC

What happened

On September 14, 1960, representatives from Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, and Venezuela met in Baghdad to establish the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC). The founding nations formed the intergovernmental cartel to coordinate petroleum policies and gain collective influence over the global oil market, which had previously been controlled by an oligopoly of dominant Anglo-American oil firms known as the Seven Sisters. Prior to this agreement, individual nations attempting to alter oil production terms faced military intervention, such as the 1953 foreign-sponsored coup against Mohammad Mosaddegh in Iran, or economic retaliation through production shifts.

The organization established its supreme authority in the OPEC Conference, composed of delegations typically led by member countries' oil ministers. Headquartered in Vienna, the conference operates on principles of unanimity and equal voting rights, while appointing a secretary general as chief executive. Saudi Arabia emerged as the de facto leader due to its position as the world's largest oil exporter and swing producer capable of balancing market supply.

What changed after

The creation of OPEC shifted authority over natural resources toward oil-producing states, facilitating the nationalization of oil production across member nations without direct retaliation from Western corporations. By 2022, OPEC member states held 79.5 percent of proven global oil reserves, with 67.2 percent of the organization's total reserves concentrated in the Middle East.

A broader coalition called OPEC+ was established in late 2016 to incorporate additional oil-producing states into market coordination efforts. The organization's membership fluctuated over time; the United Arab Emirates departed both OPEC and OPEC+ in 2026, leaving OPEC with 11 member countries.

What they did

Eduardo Luis Barrios Manzano was born in San Francisco, California, on September 14, 1955. He attended St. Paul University Manila and De La Salle University, where he competed on the judo and basketball teams under coach Joaqui Trillo. He moved to the Philippines to pursue acting full-time, serving as president of the Katipunan ng mga Artista ng Pelikulang Pilipino in the 1990s. Over his television career, he hosted game shows including The Weakest Link, Pilipinas, Game KNB?, 1 vs. 100, Asar Talo Lahat Panalo!, and Game 'N Go.

Manzano entered political office in 1998 upon election as Vice Mayor of Makati under the Liberal Party banner. Although his proclamation was delayed by a legal challenge regarding his US birthplace, the Supreme Court ruled in Mercado v. Manzano that he held Filipino citizenship. He lost the 2001 Makati mayoral election to Jejomar Binay. From February 2004 to August 2009, Manzano served as the first chairman of the Optical Media Board, directing government anti-piracy operations. He subsequently ran for Vice President of the Philippines in 2010 alongside Gilbert Teodoro, for Senate in 2016 under the Partido Galing at Puso coalition, and for San Juan representative in 2019.

Opening of the Penang Bridge

Malaysia inaugurated a 13.5-kilometer toll bridge linking the island of Penang directly to the mainland peninsula.

What happened

Construction of the Penang Bridge began in 1982 to establish a direct road link across the Penang Strait between Gelugor on Penang Island and Perai on the mainland peninsula. Completed in three years at a cost of RM 850 million, the 13.5-kilometer dual carriageway was officially inaugurated on September 14, 1985. The structure featured a 255-meter main cable-stayed span elevated 33 meters above the water, supported by four 101.5-meter towers.

Upon opening, tolls were instituted exclusively for traffic moving mainland-to-island toward Penang. In 1994, private concessionaire Penang Bridge Sdn Bhd took over toll collection operations, later integrating into PLUS Malaysia Berhad. Initially built with four lanes along most of its length and six on the central span, a widening project from January 2008 to late 2009 expanded the entire carriageway to six lanes.

What changed after

The bridge served as the initial highway connection between Penang Island and Seberang Perai on the mainland. On September 15, 1985, the Malaysian Postal Services Department issued commemorative stamps in denominations of 20 sen, 40 sen, and RM 1.00 to mark the opening.

Electronic toll collection via Touch 'n Go cards and SmartTAGs became mandatory at the Perai toll plaza on September 9, 2015. On January 1, 2019, the RM 1.40 toll fee previously charged to motorcyclists was permanently abolished.
