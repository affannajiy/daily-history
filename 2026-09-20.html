Departure of Magellan's Expedition

Ferdinand Magellan set sail from Spain with five ships in 1519 to find a western route to the Spice Islands.

What happened

Born in Portugal around 1480, Ferdinand Magellan served the Portuguese Crown in Asia, participating in the Battle of Cannanore in 1506, the Battle of Diu in 1509, and the conquest of Malacca in 1511. After falling out of favor with King Manuel I, who denied his persistent requests to lead an expedition westward to the Moluccas, Magellan left for Spain in 1517. Partnering with cosmographer Rui Faleiro, he investigated a Atlantic passage to the South Pacific under the Treaty of Tordesillas.

King Charles I of Spain approved Magellan's plan, appointing him an admiral of the Spanish fleet in 1518 and giving him command of the five-ship Armada of Molucca. Magellan led the fleet from Sanlúcar de Barrameda southwest across the Atlantic Ocean with about 270 men. The fleet navigated storms and mutinies, passed through the Strait of Magellan, and entered the ocean Magellan named the Mar Pacifico before reaching Guam and the Philippines.

What changed after

Although Magellan was killed by a poison arrow in the Battle of Mactan in April 1521, Juan Sebastián Elcano guided the single remaining ship westward across the Indian Ocean and back to Spain in September 1522. This journey demonstrated a navigable western passage around South America through the Strait of Magellan and achieved the first recorded circumnavigation of the planet.

Admission of Vietnam to the United Nations

Vietnam gained admission to the United Nations in 1977 following its reunification as a single socialist republic after long warfare.

What happened

Following the August Revolution in 1945 and the defeat of French forces at the Battle of Dien Bien Phu in 1954, the Geneva accords separated Vietnam into communist North Vietnam and anti-communist South Vietnam. Military conflict escalated between North Vietnam, supported by the Soviet Union and China, and South Vietnam, backed by the United States. The conflict ended with the fall of Saigon on April 30, 1975, leading to the formal reunification of the country as the Socialist Republic of Vietnam on July 2, 1976.

In 1977, the newly reunified Socialist Republic of Vietnam was formally admitted as a member state of the United Nations. At the time of its admission, the country faced post-war reconstruction challenges, an economic trade embargo imposed by Western nations, and ongoing regional conflicts with neighboring Cambodia and China.

What changed after

Joining the United Nations enabled Vietnam to establish formal diplomatic relations globally despite the ongoing Western trade embargo. Following the implementation of Đổi Mới economic reforms in 1986, Vietnam integrated into international trade networks, joined ASEAN in 1995, and eventually secured a non-permanent seat on the United Nations Security Council twice.
