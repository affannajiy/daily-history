First Battle of the Marne

What happened

In August 1914, the German army invaded France planning to win the war in 40 days by occupying Paris and destroying the Allied armies. Following victories at the Battles of Mons and the Frontiers, German forces overran northern France and Belgium, forcing French and British troops into the Great Retreat over 250 km southwards. By early September, German units advanced to within 40 km of Paris, prompting BEF commander Field Marshal John French to prepare plans for a retreat to English Channel ports.

French commander Joseph Joffre maintained order, reinforced his army with troops from the eastern flank, and integrated reserve units into the regular army. On September 3, Paris military governor Joseph Gallieni identified a vulnerable German right flank, leading Joffre to order a counter-offensive on September 4. Fought between Paris and Verdun across a 230 km frontline, the battle forced the German 1st and 2nd Armies to retreat 65 km to the Aisne River by September 9 to avoid encirclement.

What changed after

The German retreat to a line north of the Aisne River between September 9 and September 12 ended Germany's plan to defeat France quickly in a 40-day campaign. Both militaries subsequently engaged in reciprocal maneuvering to turn the opponent's northern flank during the Race to the Sea.

These operations culminated in the First Battle of Ypres and established four years of trench warfare across the Western Front.

Goh Eng Wah

Muar-born film distributor Goh Eng Wah built a cinema business in Singapore after surviving the Japanese occupation during World War II.

What they did

Goh Eng Wah was born in Muar, Johor, Malaya, in 1923. After graduating from secondary school, he moved to Singapore to escape the Imperial Japanese Army but lived under Japanese occupation from 1942 to 1945. His entertainment career began in 1945 when he co-opened a cinema at Victory Theatre in Gay World Amusement Park at Geylang, initially screening Japanese propaganda films before acquiring Happy Theatre. He became the sole owner two years later and began buying additional venues.

In 1966, Goh acquired Jubilee Theatre and King's Theatre in Tiong Bahru, later establishing Eng Wah Theatres Organisation Pte Ltd in 1968. During the 1970s, he expanded cinema operations into new Housing & Development Board towns, starting in Toa Payoh and expanding to Clementi, Ang Mo Kio, and Kallang Bahru. Under his direction, the firm specialized in importing Chinese movies from Hong Kong and Taiwan, becoming a leading film distributor in the 1980s and listing on the Singapore Exchange on July 4, 1994.

What still stands

Goh Eng Wah founded Eng Wah Global Pte Ltd, which listed on the Singapore Exchange. His properties include 321 Clementi, Jubilee Square, and Hotel Fort Canning.
