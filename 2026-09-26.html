What happened

In 1905 Albert Einstein published the third of his Annus Mirabilis papers in the scientific journal Annalen der Physik. The paper introduced the special theory of relativity, proclaiming the constancy of the speed of light and deriving the Lorentz transformations.

Einstein also examined relativistic aberration and the transverse Doppler effect within the same work, extending the revolutionary impact of the four 1905 papers on modern physics.

What changed after

After the 1905 publication, the constancy of light speed became an accepted principle, prompting physicists across Europe to adopt the Lorentz transformations in their theoretical work.

Experimental verification arrived with Ives and Stilwell’s time‑dilation measurements in 1938 and 1941 and Rossi and Hall’s 1941 experiments, which cemented relativity’s status in mainstream physics.
