Columbus lands on San Salvador Island

Columbus’s fleet reached Guanahani on 12 October 1492, opening European contact with the Caribbean and reshaping world trade.

What happened

Columbus left the Port of Palos in August 1492 with three ships and sailed westward under the sponsorship of Queen Isabella I and King Ferdinand II. On 12 October 1492 his fleet sighted an island in the Bahamas that the native inhabitants called Guanahani, which later became known as San Salvador Island. After landing, Columbus and his men explored the neighboring islands, moving on to the lands now called Cuba and Hispaniola and establishing a Spanish colony on the latter. He returned to Castile in early 1493, bringing captured Indigenous people and reports that spread quickly throughout Europe.

What changed after

The landing inaugurated a period of European exploration, conquest and colonization that brought the Americas into the European sphere of influence. It triggered the Columbian exchange, a massive transfer of plants, animals, peoples and diseases between the Old and New Worlds that reshaped economies and societies on both sides of the Atlantic. Subsequent Spanish crowns established permanent colonies throughout the Caribbean, Central and South America, laying the foundations for modern nation‑states in the region.

2002 Bali nightclub bombings

Suicide bombers struck Kuta’s Sari Club and Paddy’s Pub on 12 October 2002, killing 202 and wounding over 200 tourists.

Who
Jemaah Islamiyah terrorists
What
Detonated bombs at two nightclubs
Where
Sari Club and Paddy’s Pub, Kuta
When
12 October 2002, around 11:05 p.m.
What happened

On the night of 12 October 2002 three coordinated attacks hit Kuta’s tourist district. A suicide bomber named Iqbal entered Paddy’s Pub wearing an explosive‑laden vest and detonated it on the packed dance floor, killing himself and many patrons. Twenty seconds later a second bomber, Arnasan, detonated a van bomb outside the nearby Sari Club, creating a crater and killing himself along with dozens of fleeing victims. A third device, placed near the U.S. Consulate in Denpasar, was detonated remotely, causing minor damage and one injury. The blasts killed a total of 202 people—151 Western tourists and 38 Indonesians—and injured 209, making it the deadliest terrorist attack in Indonesia’s history.

What changed after

The attack prompted the United Nations Security Council to pass Resolution 1438, labeling the bombings a threat to international peace and security. Indonesian authorities intensified counter‑terrorism operations, arresting and executing dozens of Jemaah Islamiyah members, including the 2008 executions of Amrozi, Imam Samudra and Huda bin Abdul Haq. The tragedy also led to major upgrades in emergency medical response capacity and stricter security measures at tourist venues across Indonesia.
