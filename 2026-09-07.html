1822 · São Paulo, Brazil

Independence of Brazil

Prince Pedro of Braganza defied the Portuguese courts by declaring Brazil an independent country along the Ipiranga brook.

Pedro Américo — Public domain · source
Who
Prince Pedro of Braganza
What
Declared Brazil independent from Portugal
Where
Ipiranga brook, São Paulo, Brazil
When
7 September 1822
What happened

In 1807, the French army invaded Portugal, prompting the Portuguese royal family and government to relocate to Brazil. In December 1815, Prince Regent John elevated Brazil to the status of a kingdom united with Portugal. Following a liberal revolution in Portugal in 1820, King John VI returned to Lisbon and appointed his eldest son, Pedro of Braganza, as prince regent of Brazil.

Tensions escalated when the Portuguese courts ordered Pedro to return to Lisbon and reduced his role to governor-of-arms. Pedro refused and chose to rebel, launching a war of independence that began in Pernambuco in 1821. On 7 September 1822, beside the Ipiranga brook in São Paulo, Pedro delivered the declaration of independence known as the Cry of Ipiranga.

What changed after

Prince Pedro was acclaimed Emperor of Brazil on 12 October 1822 and crowned on 1 December 1822, establishing the Empire of Brazil. Armed conflict against Portuguese garrisons continued across provinces including Cisplatina, Bahia, Piauí, Maranhão, and Grão-Pará, alongside the suppression of the republican Confederation of the Equator movement in Pernambuco.

Portugal formally recognized Brazilian sovereignty on 29 August 1825 through the Treaty of Rio de Janeiro. Under the agreement, Brazil paid compensation to Portugal and concluded two treaties with the United Kingdom, agreeing to abolish the Atlantic slave trade and grant preferential import tariffs to British goods.

2021 · Myanmar

NUG Declaration of People's Defensive War

Myanmar's ousted lawmakers and opposition leaders declared a nationwide defensive war against the ruling military junta in September 2021.

Who
National Unity Government of Myanmar
What
Launched a defensive war and revolution
Where
Myanmar
When
September 2021
What happened

In the aftermath of the 2021 coup d'état in Myanmar, ousted lawmakers from the Committee Representing Pyidaungsu Hluttaw formed the National Unity Government. This coalition included members of Aung San Suu Kyi's deposed National League for Democracy, representatives of ethnic minority insurgent groups, and various minor political parties. Operating remotely and in hiding within Myanmar, the coalition sought to challenge the authority of the military junta, known as the State Administration Council.

In May 2021, the National Unity Government announced the creation of the People's Defense Force to oppose the regime. Tensions culminated in September 2021 when the shadow government officially launched a defensive war and nationwide revolution against the military junta. In response, the State Administration Council declared the shadow government an illegal terrorist organization, while opposition leaders used Section 3 of Myanmar's Counter-Terrorism Law to brand the Tatmadaw armed forces as a terrorist group.

What changed after

Following the declaration, the National Unity Government expanded its international presence by establishing representative offices in the United States, United Kingdom, Norway, France, Czech Republic, Australia, and South Korea by September 2021. The European Parliament formally recognized the coalition as the legitimate government of Myanmar, and the administration appointed Saw Ba Hla Thein as its envoy to Japan on 1 February 2022.

Despite lacking full diplomatic status in host nations, these representative offices enabled the shadow government to conduct foreign affairs while operating remotely within Myanmar. The mutual terrorist designations between the military junta and opposition forces hardened the political conflict across the country.
