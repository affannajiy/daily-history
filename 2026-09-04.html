476 · Italy

Deposition of Romulus Augustulus

The barbarian general Odoacer overthrew the child emperor Romulus Augustulus and established himself as the first barbarian King of Italy.

ANS — CC0 · source
Who
Odoacer and barbarian foederati
What
Deposed Western Roman emperor Romulus Augustulus
Where
Italy
When
September 4, 476
What happened

In October 475, the magister militum Orestes installed his minor son Romulus Augustus onto the Western Roman throne after usurping power from Emperor Julius Nepos. Nepos fled to Dalmatia and continued claiming the imperial title, preventing Eastern Roman Emperor Zeno from acknowledging Romulus's legitimacy. Operating as little more than a figurehead for his father, Romulus issued coinage bearing his full title, Dominus Noster Romulus Augustus Pius Felix Augustus, though contemporaries derisively dubbed him Augustulus.

In 476, barbarian foederati allied with Rome demanded settlements of land across Italy. When Orestes refused their requests, the foederati united under the leadership of the general Odoacer. Odoacer's forces defeated and executed Orestes, entered Italy, and deposed Romulus on September 4, 476. Rather than placing another figurehead on the throne, Odoacer proclaimed himself King of Italy and acknowledged Zeno as his nominal overlord.

What changed after

Following the deposition, Odoacer spared Romulus's life and sent him to live at castellum Lucullanum, a fortress in Campania, supported by a pension. The Western Roman Empire ceased to exist as a political entity, with authority over Italy shifting directly to Odoacer under nominal Eastern Roman oversight.

In Campania, the retired emperor remained until at least the early sixth century, receiving pension correspondence from Odoacer's successor, Theodoric the Great, in 507 or 511. The area around castellum Lucullanum also saw the establishment of a monastery dedicated to Saint Severinus of Noricum in the 480s or 490s.

Southeast Asia
1967 · Quế Sơn Valley, South Vietnam

Operation Swift

United States Marines launched Operation Swift to rescue two ambushed companies engaged in heavy combat against North Vietnamese forces.

Who
U.S. 1st Marine Division
What
Launched Operation Swift to rescue ambushed Marines
Where
Quế Sơn Valley, South Vietnam
When
September 4, 1967
What happened

In early 1967, at least two regiments of the PAVN 2nd Division infiltrated the Quế Sơn Valley along the border of Quảng Nam and Quảng Tín Provinces. Recognised by the U.S. Military Assistance Command, Vietnam as strategic ground controlling South Vietnam's five northern provinces, the valley was assigned to the 5th Marine Regiment. Marine Operations Union and Union II in spring and summer 1967 temporarily pushed PAVN forces back, but the PAVN rebuilt its presence to three regular regiments alongside the Vietcong 1st Regiment while Marine patrolling waned.

Following the limited outcome of Operation Cochise in late August 1967, two U.S. Marine companies suffered an ambush in the Quế Sơn Valley by the PAVN. On September 4, 1967, units of the U.S. 1st Marine Division launched Operation Swift to relieve the trapped infantry. Heavy fighting broke out across the valley as Marine reinforcements engaged enemy units to extract the isolated companies.

What changed after

During the fighting of Operation Swift between September 4 and September 15, 1967, U.S. forces suffered 127 Marines killed, while an estimated 600 PAVN soldiers lost their lives in the Quế Sơn Valley. The heavy engagement halted the immediate advance of the reinforced PAVN 2nd Division and the Vietcong 1st Regiment across Quảng Nam and Quảng Tín Provinces.

Control over the agricultural lands of the Quế Sơn Valley remained contested, requiring continued deployment of the 5th Marine Regiment alongside Army of the Republic of Vietnam units to protect the northern provinces of South Vietnam.
