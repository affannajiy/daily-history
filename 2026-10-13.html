Arrest of the Knights Templar

King Philip IV of France ordered dawn arrests of Knights Templar across his realm in 1307 to destroy their financial power.

What happened

King Philip IV of France, heavily indebted to the Knights Templar and viewing them as a state within the state that threatened royal authority, targeted the order in 1307. A year prior, in 1306, Philip had already expelled the Jews from France under similar financial pressures. Relying on civil servants like Guillaume de Nogaret and Enguerrand de Marigny to centralize monarchical power, the king ordered the total destruction of the military order.

Under King Philip's orders, hundreds of Templars across France were seized in dawn raids in 1307 and subsequently forced to confess to heresy under torture. This move formed part of Philip's broader effort to strip privileges from feudal and religious institutions, which had previously included an attack on Pope Boniface VIII at Anagni in September 1303 and eventually contributed to the relocation of the papal court to Avignon in 1309.

What changed after

The suppression of the Templars and expulsion of the Jewish population eliminated major creditors to the French crown, consolidating financial and legal control under King Philip IV. Royal authority replaced feudal privileges as civil servants expanded state administration across French crown lands.

The wider assault on ecclesiastical independence forced the Catholic Church into the Avignon Papacy starting in 1309. Following the deaths of Philip's three sons—Louis X, Philip V, and Charles IV—without surviving male heirs, a severe succession crisis culminated in the outbreak of the Hundred Years' War in 1337.

What they did

Born in Cambridge, Massachusetts, in 1927, Bhumibol Adulyadej was educated in Switzerland at the École nouvelle de la Suisse romande and the University of Lausanne. He ascended the Thai throne on 9 June 1946 as King Rama IX following the death of his elder brother, King Ananda Mahidol (Rama VIII). He ruled Thailand for 70 years and 126 days, becoming the longest-reigning monarch in Thai history and presiding over rapid economic growth between 1985 and 1994.

Throughout his reign, King Bhumibol managed political shifts marked by military interventions, including the 2014 military coup that ended 20 years of civilian administration. He maintained substantial wealth managed by the Crown Property Bureau, valued at 30 billion US dollars by Forbes in 2010. Following repeated hospitalizations at Siriraj Hospital, he died on 13 October 2016 and was succeeded by his son, King Rama X.

What still stands

The Crown Property Bureau continues to manage institutional royal assets and investments, while Sanam Luang remains the designated royal crematorium site where the king's official funeral ceremonies occurred in 2017. Additionally, Siriraj Hospital functions as a major medical center in Bangkok where the monarch received treatment during his final years.
