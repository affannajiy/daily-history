1846 · Berlin, Kingdom of Prussia

Discovery of Neptune

Astronomers at the Berlin Observatory identified Neptune in the night sky using mathematical predictions calculated by Urbain Le Verrier.

Carl Daniel Freydanck — Public domain · source
Who
Johann Gottfried Galle and Urbain Le Verrier
What
Discovered Neptune through mathematically-predicted observation
Where
Berlin Observatory, Berlin
When
September 23–24, 1846
What happened

In 1821, Alexis Bouvard published astronomical tables predicting the orbit of Uranus based on Newton's laws. When subsequent observations revealed deviations in its path, astronomers reasoned that the gravitational pull of an undiscovered planet was disturbing Uranus's orbit. In 1845, Urbain Le Verrier in Paris and John Couch Adams in Cambridge independently began mathematical calculations to determine the position of this unknown planet.

Working from Le Verrier's calculations, astronomer Johann Gottfried Galle, assisted by Heinrich Louis d'Arrest, conducted telescopic observations at the Berlin Observatory. On the night of September 23–24, 1846, Galle directly observed Neptune, confirming the mathematical predictions and validating Newtonian gravitational theory.

What changed after

Just seventeen days after Neptune was identified, astronomer William Lassell discovered its moon, Triton. In 1847, Sears C. Walker of the U.S. Naval Observatory identified earlier uncredited observations of Neptune made in 1795 by Michel Lefrançois de Lalande's staff at the Paris Observatory, enabling a better calculation of the planet's orbit.

George Airy announced that Adams had also predicted the planet, causing a priority dispute between British and French astronomers. The Royal Society awarded Le Verrier the Copley medal in 1846 and later granted Adams the Copley medal in 1848.

Southeast Asia
1899 · Olongapo, Philippines

Battle of Olongapo

American naval forces bombarded and landed troops in Olongapo to destroy a Filipino artillery battery threatening trade vessels.

Who
American Asiatic Squadron and Filipino forces
What
Destroyed a Filipino artillery battery using guncotton
Where
Kalaklan Point, Olongapo, Philippines
When
September 23, 1899
What happened

On September 18, 1899, Filipino forces stationed at Kalaklan Point in Olongapo fired upon the American armed transport Zafiro. In response, the protected cruiser Charleston shelled the Filipino positions with eight-inch guns, silencing the battery before withdrawing to Sangley Point.

On September 23, 1899, the American Asiatic Squadron returned to Olongapo with the monitor Baltimore, the gunboat Concord, Charleston, and Zafiro. Following a heavy naval bombardment from Baltimore's ten- and twelve-inch guns, Charleston landed 180 sailors and 70 marines. After engaging Filipino forces in the naval yard and suffering one wounded casualty, the landing party reached the battery at Kalaklan Point and destroyed it using three charges of guncotton.

What changed after

The destruction of the artillery battery at Kalaklan Point enabled American patrol ships and trade vessels to navigate the sea route past Olongapo without threat of bombardment. Emilio Aguinaldo disbanded the regular Filipino army on November 13, 1899, dividing his forces into guerrilla units.

On December 10, 1899, an American force of 90 soldiers under Major Robert Spence captured Olongapo, supported by Rear Admiral John Watson commanding the Baltimore and Oregon.
