1963 · Washington, D.C., United States

March on Washington for Jobs and Freedom

Hundreds of thousands gathered in the national capital to demand civil and economic rights for African Americans.

What happened

Organized by Bayard Rustin and A. Philip Randolph, the March on Washington for Jobs and Freedom brought together an alliance of civil rights, labor, and religious organizations on August 28, 1963. An estimated 200,000 to 300,000 people assembled in Washington, D.C., with 250,000 cited as the most common count. Observers estimated that 75 to 80 percent of the participants were Black. Walter Reuther, president of the United Auto Workers, served as the highest-ranking white organizer.

Popular singers Mahalia Jackson and Marian Anderson performed at the demonstration before movement leaders addressed the crowd. Standing in front of the Lincoln Memorial, Martin Luther King Jr. spoke last, delivering his "I Have a Dream" speech to demand an end to legalized racism and racial segregation across the country.

What changed after

The rally contributed directly to the passage of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. It was followed by the Selma voting rights movement, which generated national media coverage and led to the passage of the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

1993 Singaporean presidential election

Singapore held its first direct presidential election, placing former Deputy Prime Minister Ong Teng Cheong into the head-of-state office.

When

What happened

On August 28, 1993, Singapore held its first direct presidential election following 1991 constitutional amendments that transformed the presidency into a popularly elected position with a six-year term. Incumbent president Wee Kim Wee did not seek re-election. Former Deputy Prime Minister Ong Teng Cheong resigned his cabinet position, his parliamentary seat for Toa Payoh GRC, and his ruling People's Action Party membership to enter the race.

The election featured only two candidates after the Presidential Elections Committee issued eligibility certificates to Ong and former Accountant-General Chua Kim Yeow. Chua stood as a reluctant candidate persuaded by the government to ensure a contested election. During the 10-day campaign, Chua urged supporters not to campaign for him, appeared on television only twice, and publicly stated on polling day that Ong was the superior candidate. Ong won the election with 58.69 percent of the vote against Chua's 41.3 percent.

What changed after

Ong Teng Cheong assumed office on September 1, 1993, becoming Singapore's fifth president and the first to be directly elected by popular vote. Under the 1991 constitutional revisions, the president gained veto powers over spending national reserves, key public service appointments, and security orders under the Internal Security Act.
